
# Investment Philosophies of Duan Yongping, Warren Buffett, and Charlie Munger

## 1. Duan Yongping (段永平) — The "Chinese Buffett" with an Entrepreneur's Lens

Duan Yongping is a Chinese-American billionaire entrepreneur and investor. He is the founder of BBK Electronics (which owns the OPPO and vivo smartphone brands) and a former entrepreneur behind Subor (Xiao Ba Wang) and Bu Bu Gao. He is often called "the Chinese Buffett" for his value-oriented, long-term investment style, and he is one of the most prominent individual investors in China.

### 1.1 Core Philosophy: "Buying a Stock Is Buying a Company"

Duan's single most important investment principle is simple in concept but difficult in execution: **"Buying a stock is buying a company."** He believes that fewer than 1% of people truly understand this sentence. To him, investing is not about trading ticker symbols or predicting price movements — it is about understanding the underlying business, its future cash flows, and its intrinsic value.  
*Source: [The Momentum—Duan Yongping: Value Investing Through an Entrepreneur’s Lens](https://www.the-momentum.net/p/duan-yongping-value-investing-through)*

### 1.2 Entrepreneurial Perspective

Duan's investment philosophy is uniquely shaped by his background as a successful entrepreneur. Unlike Wall Street analysts who focus on spreadsheets, he examines a company through the lens of an operator: he asks whether the business is "doing the right thing," whether its corporate culture is sound, and whether it has a sustainable competitive advantage.  
*Source: [The Momentum—Duan Yongping: Value Investing Through an Entrepreneur’s Lens](https://www.the-momentum.net/p/duan-yongping-value-investing-through)*

### 1.3 Long-Term Holding and Patience

Duan famously advocates: **"Select stocks for a year, and hold them for ten years."** He advises investors to disregard short-term swings and focus on the distant horizon. He has held Apple stock since 2011 and still holds it today. He believes that making 20 investment decisions in a lifetime is enough, and that trading frequently is a losing game.  
*Source: [Medium—The Investment Principles of Duan Yongping (Part 2)](https://medium.com/@dkjunegm/money-the-investment-principles-of-duan-yongping-the-buffett-of-china-part-2-b92c8c72e4cd)*  
*Source: [Zhihu—Some netizens have hyped up Duan Yongping](https://www.zhihu.com/en/answer/1917345996922414012)*

### 1.4 "Right Business, Right People"

Duan's investment decisions are guided by two criteria: finding the **right business** (with a great business model and durable competitive advantage) and following the **right people** (management with integrity, user-centric culture). He respects Apple's management under Steve Jobs and Tim Cook, Tencent's Pony Ma, and Kweichow Moutai's commitment to product quality.  
*Source: [Tianpan—Duan Yongping's Business Ideas: Analysis of Three Core Concepts](https://tianpan.co/notes/2025-02-15-duan-yongping-three-core-business-ideas)*

### 1.5 Margin of Safety Defined Differently

Duan reframes "margin of safety" not as how cheap a stock is, but as **how much you understand the business**. In his view, the real risk is not the stock price dropping but the risk of not understanding what you own.  
*Source: [LinkedIn—Duan Yongping shares insights on investing and corporate culture](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tengjiajimmyli_duan-yongping-a-chinese-american-billionaire-activity-7394915600617099264-s6EF)*

### 1.6 No Shortcuts, Intellectual Humility

Duan emphasizes that there are **no shortcuts in investing**. He warns against speculating, chasing insider information, or trying to time the market. He frequently says "I don't know" and stresses intellectual humility. For the average investor, he recommends buying the S&P 500 index or Berkshire Hathaway rather than trading individual stocks.  
*Source: [YouTube—Duan Yongping Breaks 20+ Years of Silence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTd0CdpnD8Y)*

### 1.7 Portfolio Concentration

As of Q1 2026, Duan's 13F portfolio (held through H&H International Investment) is heavily concentrated:  
- Apple (AAPL): ~36.7%  
- Berkshire Hathaway (BRK-B): ~21.9%  
- NVIDIA (NVDA): ~12.1%  
- PDD Holdings: ~10.1%  
- Tesla (TSLA): ~6.3%  
Total portfolio value: approximately $20 billion across 19 securities.  
*Source: [Valuesider—Duan Yongping Portfolio (2026 Q1)](https://valuesider.com/guru/duan-yongping-h-h-international-investment/portfolio)*

## 2. Warren Buffett — The Oracle of Omaha

Warren Buffett, chairman and CEO of Berkshire Hathaway, is widely regarded as the most successful investor of the 20th and 21st centuries. His philosophy is rooted in the value investing tradition of Benjamin Graham, but evolved significantly under the influence of Charlie Munger.

### 2.1 Core Philosophy: Value Investing with a Focus on Intrinsic Value

Buffett's investment philosophy is built on the concept of **intrinsic value** — the true worth of a business based on its future cash flows. He seeks to buy securities that are trading at a discount to their intrinsic value, providing a **margin of safety**. As he famously said, "Price is what you pay; value is what you get."  
*Source: [Investopedia—Warren Buffett's Value Investing Strategy Explained](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/01/071801.asp)*

### 2.2 "Buy Wonderful Companies at Fair Prices"

Early in his career, Buffett practiced "cigar-butt investing" — buying cheap, mediocre companies. Charlie Munger convinced him to shift to buying **wonderful businesses at fair prices** rather than fair businesses at wonderful prices. This is arguably the most important evolution in Buffett's philosophy.  
*Source: [Investopedia—Warren Buffett's Value Investing Strategy for Wealth](https://www.investopedia.com/warren-buffett-value-investing-strategy-patience-discipline-long-term-growth-12033085)*

### 2.3 Economic Moat

Buffett looks for companies with a durable competitive advantage — an **"economic moat"** that protects the business from competitors. This moat can be a strong brand (Coca-Cola), a network effect (American Express), low-cost production (GEICO), or high switching costs.  
*Source: [Mesirow—Timeless Lessons from Warren Buffett](https://www.mesirow.com/wealth-insights-hub/timeless-lessons-from-warren-buffett)*

### 2.4 Long-Term Horizon and Compounding

Buffett's holding period is effectively **forever**. He once said, "I never attempt to make money on the stock market. I buy on the assumption that they could close the market the next day and not reopen it for five years." He believes that time is the friend of the wonderful business, and that compounding works best when you let great companies grow over decades.  
*Source: [Investopedia—Value Investing Definition](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/v/valueinvesting.asp)*

### 2.5 Circle of Competence

Buffett and Munger both emphasize staying within your **circle of competence**. Buffett only invests in businesses he can understand deeply. He famously avoided technology stocks for decades because he didn't understand them, only investing in Apple later after recognizing it as a consumer products company.  
*Source: [Simply Ethical—Warren Buffett's Investment Tenets](https://simplyethical.com/blog/warren-buffetts-investment-tenets)*

### 2.6 Rule #1: Don't Lose Money

Buffett's first rule of investing is **"Don't lose money."** His second rule is: "Never forget rule #1." This is not about avoiding all losses, but about preserving capital and avoiding permanent impairment of capital through speculative or fundamentally flawed investments.  
*Source: [Investopedia—Warren Buffett's Value Investing Strategy Explained](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/01/071801.asp)*

### 2.7 Key Portfolio Holdings (Berkshire Hathaway)

Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio (as of mid-2025) is concentrated in:  
- Apple (AAPL)  
- American Express (AXP)  
- Coca-Cola (KO)  
- Bank of America (BAC)  
- Occidental Petroleum (OXY)  
- Chevron (CVX)  

Berkshire itself is a conglomerate owning GEICO, BNSF Railway, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, and many other wholly-owned businesses.  
*Source: [Investopedia—Warren Buffett's Value Investing Strategy for Wealth](https://www.investopedia.com/warren-buffett-value-investing-strategy-patience-discipline-long-term-growth-12033085)*

## 3. Charlie Munger — The Architect of Berkshire's Philosophy

Charlie Munger, vice-chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, was Warren Buffett's long-time partner and the intellectual architect behind many of Berkshire's most important strategic shifts. He passed away in November 2024 at age 99.

### 3.1 Core Philosophy: The Latticework of Mental Models

Munger's most distinctive contribution is the concept of **"mental models"** — a multidisciplinary toolkit of frameworks from psychology, physics, biology, economics, history, and other fields. He argued that you cannot make good decisions with just one or two models; you need a "latticework" of models to understand the complex reality of business and investing. He famously said: "To a man with a hammer, everything looks like a nail."  
*Source: [ModelThinkers—Munger's Latticework](https://modelthinkers.com/mental-model/mungers-latticework)*  
*Source: [OneBridge Wealth—Charlie Munger Mental Models for Smarter Investing](https://www.onebridgewealth.com/blog/charlie-mungers-mental-models-the-framework-behind-smarter-investing)*

### 3.2 Inversion: "Invert, Always Invert"

Munger's most famous mental model is **inversion**. Instead of asking "How do I achieve success?", he asked "How do I avoid failure?" By focusing on the mistakes that could destroy wealth, he and Buffett built systems to avoid them. This is the philosophical foundation behind Buffett's "Don't lose money" rule.  
*Source: [Rule #1 Investing—Charlie Munger Investing Rules](https://www.ruleoneinvesting.com/blog/personal-development/5-charlie-munger-investing-lessons-every-value-investor-should-live-by)*

### 3.3 Circle of Competence and Intellectual Humility

Munger stressed that you must know the boundaries of your own knowledge. **"Acknowledging what you don't know is the dawning of wisdom."** He believed that investors should only commit capital to businesses they deeply understand, and that most people would be better off not trying to beat the market.  
*Source: [Sloww—Investing Principles Checklist of Charlie Munger](https://www.sloww.co/investing-principles-checklist-charlie-munger)*

### 3.4 Quality over Cheapness: The Shift to "Wonderful Companies at Fair Prices"

While Benjamin Graham taught Buffett to buy cheap, **Munger taught Buffett to buy quality**. He argued that a wonderful business purchased at a fair price would outperform a mediocre business purchased at a cheap price over the long run. This insight transformed Berkshire's approach and is the single most important intellectual contribution Munger made to Buffett's evolution.  
*Source: [The Motley Fool—Charlie Munger's Investment Strategy](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/charlie-munger)*

### 3.5 Patience and the Power of Compounding

Munger believed that the big money is made by **waiting**, not by trading. He said, "The big money is not in the buying and the selling, but in the waiting." He held stocks for decades and let compounding work its magic.  
*Source: [The Motley Fool—Charlie Munger's Investment Strategy](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/charlie-munger)*

### 3.6 The Psychology of Human Misjudgment

Munger was a pioneer in applying **behavioral psychology** to investing. He identified about 25 cognitive biases that cause humans to make poor decisions, such as confirmation bias, social proof, and the anchoring effect. He believed that avoiding stupidity is more important than seeking brilliance.  
*Source: [ModelThinkers—Munger's Latticework](https://modelthinkers.com/mental-model/mungers-latticework)*

### 3.7 Concentrated Portfolio

Munger was a strong advocate of **concentration** over diversification. He argued that you can't get rich by owning a little bit of everything. When you find a great idea, you should bet heavily. This is reflected in Berkshire's high-conviction portfolio and Duan Yongping's similar approach.  
*Source: [OneBridge Wealth—Charlie Munger Mental Models](https://www.onebridgewealth.com/blog/charlie-mungers-mental-models-the-framework-behind-smarter-investing)*

## 4. Commonalities and Differences

| Principle | Duan Yongping | Warren Buffett | Charlie Munger |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Core Idea** | Buying a stock = buying a company | Intrinsic value + margin of safety | Latticework of mental models |
| **Business Quality** | "Right business, right people" | Wonderful companies at fair prices | Quality over cheapness |
| **Time Horizon** | Buy for 10+ years, hold forever | Hold forever; buy as if market will close | "Big money is in the waiting" |
| **Risk Management** | Understand the business deeply | Margin of safety; don't lose money | Inversion; avoid stupidity |
| **Portfolio** | Concentrated (10-20 stocks) | Concentrated (Berkshire's top 5) | Highly concentrated |
| **Advice for Average Investor** | Buy S&P 500 or Berkshire | Buy low-cost index funds | Buy index funds; don't trade |
| **Intellectual Humility** | "I don't know" is a key phrase | "Circle of competence" | "Know what you don't know" |
| **Influence on Each Other** | Explicitly follows Buffett-Munger school | Munger shifted him from "cigar-butts" to quality | Munger was the intellectual architect |

---

## 5. Conclusion

The investment philosophies of Duan Yongping, Warren Buffett, and Charlie Munger are deeply interconnected. All three are value investors who believe in buying businesses, not stocks; holding for the long term; concentrating on your best ideas; staying within your circle of competence; and avoiding speculation. Duan Yongping explicitly acknowledges the influence of Buffett and Munger, and his philosophy is a practical application of their principles within the context of the Chinese market and technology sector.

The key evolution in this school of thought — from Benjamin Graham's "buy cheap" to the Buffett-Munger "buy wonderful" — was driven by Charlie Munger. Buffett provided the capital allocation discipline and the public face. Duan Yongping provided a living example of how these principles work when applied by an entrepreneur-investor with deep operational understanding.
