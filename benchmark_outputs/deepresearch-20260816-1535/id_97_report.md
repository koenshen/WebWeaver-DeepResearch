

# Socioeconomic Impacts of a 500,000-Passenger Airport: An Evidence-Based Assessment

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The hypothesis that an airport handling 500,000 annual passengers can generate significant and measurable socioeconomic impacts on its surrounding region is **strongly supported** by the available evidence. Multiple peer-reviewed studies, government-commissioned reports, and real-world airport economic impact assessments consistently demonstrate that airports at this scale produce meaningful economic output, support hundreds to thousands of jobs, generate tax revenues, attract business investment, and catalyze broader regional development. The evidence is particularly robust for small-hub and non-hub airports in the United States and comparable regional airports in Europe, many of which operate at or near the 500,000-passenger threshold.

However, the evidence also reveals important caveats: the magnitude of the impact depends on regional economic structure, the degree of economic "leakage," the airport's operational focus (e.g., origin-destination vs. connecting traffic, leisure vs. business), and local policy support. Negative externalities—noise, air quality, and potential social disruption—are documented but are generally outweighed by the measurable economic benefits in most empirical studies.

---

## 2. Defining the Benchmark: The 500,000-Passenger Airport

In the FAA's classification system, an airport with 500,000 annual passengers falls squarely within the **non-hub primary** or **small hub** category. According to the FAA:

- **Non-hub primary**: 10,000 to 0.05% of total U.S. annual enplanements (roughly 10,000–450,000 enplanements, or ~20,000–900,000 total passengers).
- **Small hub**: 0.05% to 0.25% of total U.S. enplanements (roughly 450,000–2.25 million enplanements, or ~900,000–4.5 million passengers).

An airport with 500,000 total passengers (~250,000 enplanements) sits at the lower end of the small-hub range or the upper end of the non-hub range. This is a critical size class where airports transition from purely local service to having broader regional economic significance.

## 3. Quantitative Evidence: Direct Economic Impacts

### 3.1 Employment Generation

**ACRP Research Report 218** (Ballard et al., 2020) provides specific job-per-seat estimates for airports of this scale:

- **Small-hub airports**: 0.005 jobs per annual departing seat (5 jobs per 1,000 seats).
- **Non-hub airports**: 0.0114 jobs per annual departing seat (11.4 jobs per 1,000 seats)—more than double the small-hub rate.

For a 500,000-passenger airport with a typical load factor of ~80%, this translates to approximately 312,500 departing seats, supporting roughly **1,560–3,560 regional jobs** depending on hub classification.

**Source**: [ACRP Research Report 218 - The Role of Aviation in Supporting Local Economic Activity](https://crp.trb.org/acrpwebresource12/understanding-air-service-and-regional-economic-activity/the-role-of-aviation-in-supporting-local-economic-activity)

**Airports Council International (ACI) Europe** analysis found that every 1,000 passengers is associated with an average of 0.954 direct jobs. For 500,000 passengers, this yields approximately **477 direct on-airport jobs**, with additional indirect and induced employment multiplying the total.

**Source**: [PMC - Criticality of a Regional Airport Development](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8783399)

**The National Academies** synthesis of economic impact studies for U.S. commercial airports reports that, on average:

- **100 additional enplanements produce one additional job in the region**.
- For 500,000 passengers (~250,000 enplanements), this implies approximately **2,500 total regional jobs** supported.

**Source**: [National Academies - Chapter 4: The Role of the Airport in the Local Economy](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28759/chapter/5)

### 3.2 Income and Output

The same National Academies source provides marginal economic impact estimates:

- **One additional enplanement produces $437 in annual income** in the region. For 250,000 enplanements, this implies **~$109 million in annual regional income**.
- **One additional enplanement produces $1,299 in additional regional output**. For 250,000 enplanements, this implies **~$325 million in annual regional output**.

**Source**: [National Academies - Chapter 4: The Role of the Airport in the Local Economy](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28759/chapter/5)

### 3.3 General Aviation (GA) Activity

For airports with significant GA activity, the National Academies also reports:

- **183 additional GA operations produce one job** in the region.
- Each additional GA operation produces **$231 in regional income** and **$657 in additional regional output**.
- An additional based jet at a GA airport results in **14 added jobs, $666,126 in added income, and over $2.1 million in additional regional output**.

**Source**: [National Academies - Chapter 4: The Role of the Airport in the Local Economy](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28759/chapter/5)

## 4. Case Study Evidence: Airports at Comparable Scale

### 4.1 Rapid City Regional Airport (RAP) — 338,458 Passengers

The 2023 economic impact study for Rapid City Regional Airport provides a near-perfect analog for a 500,000-passenger facility. With 338,458 commercial passengers in 2022:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total annual economic activity | **$456 million** |
| Total employment supported | **2,883 jobs** |
| Tax revenue to Pennington County | **$2.2 million** |
| Commercial visitor direct spending | **$153 million** |
| Commercial visitor economic impact | **$188 million** |
| Airport business tenants impact | **$196 million** |
| Implied output per passenger | **~$1,347** |

The study used intercept surveys of 7,537 passengers to measure spending patterns, ensuring that only "new money" from non-local visitors was counted. Scaling linearly to 500,000 passengers would suggest approximately **$674 million in total economic activity** and **~4,250 jobs**.

**Source**: [Rapid City Regional Airport - 2023 Economic Impact Study](https://rapairport.com/2023-economic-impact-study)

### 4.2 Eugene Airport (EUG) — ~607,731 Visitors

The 2024 Eugene Airport economic impact study, conducted by Martin Associates, found:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total business revenue | **$474.2 million** |
| Total jobs supported | **10,383** |
| Direct on-site jobs | 771 (avg. salary $52,659) |
| Visitor-supported jobs | 9,157 |
| Total personal income | **$441.6 million** |
| State & local taxes | **$42.9 million** |
| Federal aviation-specific taxes | **$39.0 million** |

The study noted that 95% of direct jobs are held by residents of Lane County, confirming strong local retention of economic benefits.

**Source**: [Eugene Airport - 2024 Economic Impact Study Executive Summary](https://www.eugene-or.gov/DocumentCenter/View/75885/EUG-Economic-Impact-Study-Executive-Summary-2024)

### 4.3 McAllen International Airport (MFE) — Passenger Data

McAllen International Airport's 2024 economic impact study reported:

- **$1.2 billion in total economic output**
- **8,419 direct jobs**
- **$356 million in payroll economic impact**
- Each new flight can generate **$10 million in direct economic impact per year**

**Source**: [McAllen International Airport Economic Impact Study](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6386907/texas/hidalgo-county/mcallen/mcallen-international-airport-study-12-billion-total-economic-impact-8400-direct-jobs-in-2024-city-says)

### 4.4 European Regional Airport Case Study: Crete (Kasteli)

The PMC study on a new regional airport in Crete, Greece provides detailed input-output modeling for a facility expected to handle 8–12 million passengers. While this is much larger than 500,000, the study's methodology and job-per-passenger ratios are instructive. The ACI benchmark of 0.954 direct jobs per 1,000 passengers was validated, and the study found that regional airports in tourist-dependent areas can have disproportionately large catalytic effects on the local economy.

**Source**: [PMC - Criticality of a Regional Airport Development](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8783399)

## 5. Broader Socioeconomic Effects

### 5.1 Employment and Population Growth

**McGraw (2020)** conducted a 60-year longitudinal analysis (1950–2010) of airports in midsized and smaller cities. The findings are directly relevant:

- Presence of airports contributed to an average **3.9% growth in total employment per decade**.
- **3.4% growth in population per decade**.
- Effects on wages and job creation in airport cities were also statistically significant.

**Source**: [ACRP Web Resource - The Role of Aviation in Supporting Local Economic Activity](https://crp.trb.org/acrpwebresource12/understanding-air-service-and-regional-economic-activity/the-role-of-aviation-in-supporting-local-economic-activity)

### 5.2 Connectivity and Business Attraction

**Bilotkach (2015)** found that the number of destinations served by non-stop flights has a positive impact on total jobs, number of business establishments, and average wages. Adding flights to a new destination generates more economic effects than adding capacity to an existing destination. On average, connecting an MSA with **an extra destination created 223 jobs and 15 new business establishments**.

**Bloningen and Cristea (2015)** found that a **50% increase in the air passenger growth rate** leads to:

- **3.2% increase** in annual per-capita income growth
- **5.5% increase** in annual employment growth rate

**Source**: [ACRP Web Resource](https://crp.trb.org/acrpwebresource12/understanding-air-service-and-regional-economic-activity/the-role-of-aviation-in-supporting-local-economic-activity)

### 5.3 Catalytic and Multiplier Effects

The **ACI Europe 2024 study** on the socioeconomic impact of European airports found that each 10% increase in direct air connectivity is correlated with:

- **14% reduction** in population living in poverty
- **9% increase** in quality education
- **19% increase** in gender equality
- **8.5% increase** in research and development

At the macroeconomic level, a **10% increase in airport connectivity** yields:

- **+4.7%** increase in foreign direct investment
- **+0.5%** rise in regional GDP
- **~1%** growth in employment

**Source**: [ACI Europe - New Study on Economic and Social Impact](https://www.aci-europe.org/media-room/513-new-study-shows-airports-air-connectivity-power-5-of-european-gdp-also-supporting-quality-education-gender-equality-r-d-and-well-being.html)

### 5.4 Return on Investment

The **National Association of State Aviation Officials (NASAO)** and **American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials (AASHTO)** joint report found that for **every dollar invested in general aviation airports, $75 is returned** on average by companies utilizing those facilities.

**Source**: [Goodwyn Mills Cawood - The Surprising Impact of Airports in Smaller Communities](https://www.gmcnetwork.com/news/2025/01/the-surprising-impact-of-airports-in-smaller-communities)

### 5.5 Fiscal Impacts

Airports generate significant tax revenue at multiple levels of government. The Rapid City case study shows **$2.2 million in county-level tax revenue** from 338,000 passengers. The Eugene Airport study documented **$42.9 million in state and local taxes** and **$39.0 million in federal aviation-specific taxes**. The New York State airport system (2019) generated **$6.1 billion in tax revenue** across 111 million passengers, or approximately **$55 per passenger**.

**Source**: [NYSDOT - Economic Impacts of New York State Airports (2024)](https://www.dot.ny.gov/divisions/operating/opdm/aviation/repository/Economic%20Impacts%20of%20Airports%20and%20Aviation%206-11-2024%20(Final).pdf)

## 6. Evidence Refuting or Qualifying the Hypothesis

### 6.1 Diminishing Returns at Smaller Scales

A study by **Pot & Koster**, published in the *Journal of Air Transport Management* (2021), analyzed the relationship between air accessibility and regional economic development across Europe, with a focus on smaller airports. Key findings:

- **The contribution of smaller airports to air accessibility appears to be limited in Europe**.
- **The relationship between air transport and economic growth is stronger for large airports than for smaller airports**.
- **Peripheral regions with lower densities are more dependent on small airports**, but this dependency primarily reflects a need for connectivity rather than a guarantee of economic growth.
- **Causality mainly runs from GDP to air accessibility** for smaller airports—meaning that economically growing regions attract air service, rather than air service independently driving growth.

This is a critical qualification: a 500,000-passenger airport in a region with an already strong economic base may act as a catalyst, but the same airport in a struggling region may not, by itself, generate transformative impacts.

**Source**: [ScienceDirect - Small airports: Runways to regional economic growth?](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096669232100315X)

### 6.2 Economic Leakage

The National Academies report notes that **"the larger and more integrated the local and regional economies are, the less 'leakage' there is"** when counting indirect and induced impacts. For a 500,000-passenger airport in a small or rural region, a significant portion of the multiplier effect may leak out to larger urban centers, reducing the actual local impact. This is particularly relevant for specialized services (engineering, legal, financial) that may be sourced externally.

**Source**: [National Academies - Chapter 4](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28759/chapter/5)

### 6.3 Negative Externalities

Documented negative impacts of regional airports include:

- **Noise pollution**: Can reduce property values and desirability of surrounding areas (Kramer, 2004).
- **Air quality degradation**: From aircraft and ground traffic emissions.
- **Social disruption**: The Dhoho Airport case study in Indonesia found that airport development contributed to **"declining cooperation, reflecting a shift toward individualistic values"** and cultural changes in buffer villages.
- **Operational losses**: Small airports often struggle to break even due to low throughput volumes and limited non-aeronautical revenue (ResearchGate, small regional airports study).

**Source**: [Society Journal - Socio-Economic and Cultural Impacts of Dhoho Airport](https://societyfisipubb.id/index.php/society/article/view/704); [ResearchGate - Small Regional Airports Operation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322914274_Small_regional_airports_operation_unnecessary_burdens_or_key_to_regional_development)

### 6.4 Threat of Service Loss

The **Regional Airline Association (RAA)** notes that **150–200 communities** are in danger of losing frequency or all air service due to pilot shortages and airline profitability pressures. For a 500,000-passenger airport, the sudden loss of service would represent a significant economic shock, potentially reversing the gains documented above.

**Source**: [RAA - Valuable: Air Service to Small Communities](https://raa.org/valuable-service-air-service-to-small-communities-generates-significant-economic-activity)

## 7. Synthesis: Addressing the Hypothesis

### For the Hypothesis (Supporting Evidence)

| Evidence Type | Source | Key Finding |
|---------------|--------|-------------|
| Marginal impact per passenger | National Academies | 100 enplanements = 1 job; $437 income per enplanement |
| Jobs per departing seat | ACRP Report 218 | 0.005–0.0114 jobs per seat (500–1,140 jobs per 100,000 seats) |
| Direct jobs per 1,000 pax | ACI Europe | 0.954 direct jobs per 1,000 pax (~477 jobs for 500k) |
| Case study: RAP | Rapid City (338k pax) | $456M economic activity, 2,883 jobs |
| Case study: EUG | Eugene Airport (607k visitors) | $474M revenue, 10,383 jobs, $42.9M state/local taxes |
| Long-term employment growth | McGraw (2020) | 3.9% employment growth per decade from airport presence |
| Connectivity & FDI | ACI Europe | 10% connectivity increase → 4.7% FDI increase |
| Return on investment | NASAO/AASHTO | $75 returned per $1 invested in GA airports |
| Business attraction | Bilotkach (2015) | 223 jobs and 15 new businesses per new destination |

### Against the Hypothesis (Qualifying Evidence)

| Evidence Type | Source | Key Finding |
|---------------|--------|-------------|
| Limited impact of small airports | Pot & Koster (2021) | Smaller airports have weaker link to GDP growth; causality may run from GDP to air service |
| Economic leakage | National Academies | Leakage reduces multiplier effects in small regions |
| Negative externalities | Multiple sources | Noise, air quality, cultural disruption |
| Operational sustainability | ResearchGate | Small airports often struggle to break even |
| Service vulnerability | RAA | 150–200 communities at risk of losing air service |

## 8. Conclusion

The weight of the evidence **supports the hypothesis** that an airport handling 500,000 annual passengers can generate significant and measurable socioeconomic impacts on its surrounding region. The specific impacts are:

- **Economic output**: $300–$700 million in total annual economic activity (direct, indirect, induced, and catalytic).
- **Employment**: 1,500–10,000 total regional jobs supported, depending on the region's economic structure and the airport's mix of commercial, general aviation, and cargo activity.
- **Income**: $100–$450 million in annual regional personal income.
- **Tax revenue**: $2–$45 million annually across local, state, and federal levels.
- **Catalytic effects**: Measurable improvements in business attraction, tourism, foreign direct investment, and long-term employment and population growth.

However, the hypothesis must be **qualified** in important ways:

1. **Context matters**: The impact is larger in regions with strong economic foundations and lower in regions with high economic leakage.
2. **Causality is bidirectional**: Airports facilitate growth but are not a sufficient condition for it; underlying regional economic strength is a prerequisite.
3. **Negative externalities exist**: Noise, air quality, and social disruption are real and must be mitigated.
4. **Scale sensitivity**: The per-passenger economic impact is generally lower at smaller airports than at larger hubs, though non-hub airports show higher job-per-seat multipliers than small hubs.

The most defensible conclusion is that a 500,000-passenger airport is a **significant but not transformative** economic asset. It functions as a meaningful economic engine that, when combined with supportive regional policies, infrastructure, and economic conditions, can generate substantial and measurable benefits for its surrounding region.
