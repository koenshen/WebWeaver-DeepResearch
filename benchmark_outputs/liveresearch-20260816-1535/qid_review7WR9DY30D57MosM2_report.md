
# Spatial Understanding and Reasoning in Vision-Language Models: A Literature Review

## 1. Introduction

Spatial understanding—the ability to perceive, reason about, and act upon the arrangement of objects and scenes in 2D and 3D space—is a cornerstone of human cognition and a critical capability for embodied AI. Over the past four years (2022–2025), the vision-language model (VLM) community has made significant strides in diagnosing, benchmarking, and enhancing spatial reasoning. This review traces the evolution of this subfield through three interconnected threads: (i) the diagnosis of fundamental spatial deficits in VLMs, (ii) architectural innovations that endow models with 2D and 3D spatial grounding, and (iii) the emergence of comprehensive benchmarks that isolate and measure spatial reasoning. We cover only papers published in top-tier conferences and journals (ICML, NeurIPS, ICLR, CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, EMNLP, ACL, AAAI, TPAMI, IJCV).

## 2. The Awakening: Diagnosing the Spatial Blindness of VLMs

The first wave of research systematically exposed the surprisingly poor spatial reasoning capabilities of otherwise powerful VLMs. Kamath et al. (2023) [1] at EMNLP 2023 curated three controlled corpora—COCO-prep, GQA-prep, and RealCLEVR—that isolate spatial relation understanding by varying only the preposition (e.g., "dog under table" vs. "dog on table") while keeping object identity fixed. Evaluating 18 VLMs, they found that BLIP, which nears human parity on VQAv2, achieved only 56% accuracy against 99% for humans. Crucially, they traced the root cause to the training data: LAION-2B, the pretraining corpus for OpenCLIP, contains little reliable spatial supervision. Simple interventions like up-weighting prepositions or fine-tuning on these corpora proved insufficient, revealing a structural deficiency in how VLMs learn spatial relationships.

Building on this diagnosis, Wang et al. (2024) [2] at NeurIPS 2024 introduced SpatialEval, a benchmark spanning relational reasoning, navigation, and counting. Their evaluation of competitive LLMs and VLMs produced three counter-intuitive findings: (1) spatial reasoning remains so challenging that models can fall behind random guessing; (2) VLMs often underperform their LLM counterparts despite access to visual input; and (3) multimodal models become less reliant on visual information when sufficient textual clues are present, suggesting a modality-weighting bias. These results collectively established spatial reasoning as a persistent open problem, motivating a wave of architectural and data-centric solutions.

## 3. Architectural Innovations for 2D Spatial Grounding and Referential Reasoning

A parallel line of work focused on architectural modifications that enable VLMs to explicitly ground language in image regions. The BLIP-2 framework (Li et al., 2023) [3] at ICML 2023 introduced the Querying Transformer (Q-Former), a lightweight module that bridges a frozen image encoder and a frozen LLM via learnable queries, achieving state-of-the-art performance on vision-language tasks with 54× fewer trainable parameters than Flamingo-80B. InstructBLIP (Dai et al., 2023) [4] at NeurIPS 2023 extended this paradigm with instruction tuning, gathering 26 datasets and introducing an instruction-aware Q-Former that extracts task-relevant visual features. While not explicitly designed for spatial reasoning, these architectures established the modular connector paradigm that later spatial models would build upon.

The Ferret model (You et al., 2024) [5] at ICLR 2024 (Spotlight) made a direct leap into fine-grained spatial understanding by proposing a hybrid region representation that marries discrete coordinates with continuous visual features. This enables Ferret to accept referring inputs of any shape—points, boxes, or free-form regions—and accurately ground open-vocabulary descriptions. The accompanying GRIT dataset (1.1M samples) and Ferret-Bench benchmark provided a comprehensive evaluation of referring and grounding capabilities. Concurrently, CoVLM (Li et al., 2024) [6] at ICLR 2024 addressed compositional spatial reasoning through a communicative decoding mechanism that explicitly composes visual entities and their relationships. By dynamically routing information between vision and language modules, CoVLM achieved large gains (e.g., ~20% mAP improvement on HICO-DET for human-object interaction detection), demonstrating that explicit compositional reasoning is key to spatial understanding.

The code-generation paradigm offers an alternative route to spatial reasoning. ViperGPT (Surís et al., 2023) [7] at ICCV 2023 leverages GPT-3 Codex to generate Python code that composes vision-and-language models into subroutines for answering any visual query. By decomposing "how many muffins are on the plate?" into detection, counting, and verification steps, ViperGPT achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot results on visual question answering and grounded reasoning without any fine-tuning. VisionLLM (Wang et al., 2023) [8] at NeurIPS 2023 similarly treats images as a "foreign language," aligning vision-centric tasks with language instructions via an LLM-based decoder, achieving over 60% mAP on COCO object detection. These works demonstrate that programmatic decomposition and unified language interfaces can substantially improve spatial reasoning.

## 4. Scaling to 3D: Data-Driven Spatial Reasoning and Depth-Aware Architectures

The most significant recent advances target the 3D spatial reasoning gap—the inability of standard VLMs to reason about metric distances, object sizes, and depth relationships. SpatialVLM (Chen et al., 2024) [9] at CVPR 2024 proposed a scalable solution: an automatic 3D spatial VQA data generation framework that lifts 2D images into metric-scale 3D point clouds, producing the first Internet-scale 3D spatial reasoning dataset (2 billion VQA examples from 10 million images). Co-training a VLM on this data yields dramatic improvements: SpatialVLM outputs quantitative distance estimates within 0.5×–2× of human ground truth 37.2% of the time, and unlocks chain-of-thought multi-step reasoning (e.g., verifying whether three objects form an equilateral triangle) and robotics applications like dense reward annotation.

SpatialRGPT (Cheng et al., 2024) [10] at NeurIPS 2024 takes a complementary architectural approach, integrating depth information into existing VLMs via a flexible plugin module. The key innovations are a data curation pipeline that learns regional representations from 3D scene graphs and a depth-to-language connector that processes depth maps using the same image encoder. SpatialRGPT accurately perceives relative directions and distances from user-specified region proposals, and the accompanying SpatialRGPT-Bench provides ground-truth 3D annotations across indoor, outdoor, and simulated environments. SpatialPIN (Ma et al., 2024) [11] at NeurIPS 2024 offers a training-free alternative: it progressively prompts VLMs and interacts with 2D/3D foundation models to enhance spatial reasoning at inference time, treating these models as a "free lunch" for spatial understanding. This zero-shot, plug-and-play approach demonstrates that even without spatial fine-tuning, existing VLMs can be coaxed into better spatial reasoning through structured prompting.

On the 3D-native front, 3D-LLM (Hong et al., 2023) [12] at NeurIPS 2023 (Spotlight) injects 3D point clouds directly into large language models, using a 3D feature extractor that processes rendered multi-view images and three types of prompting mechanisms. The model handles a diverse set of 3D tasks—captioning, dense captioning, 3D QA, grounding, navigation, and task decomposition—trained on over 300k 3D-language data samples. This work established that explicit 3D feature injection is a viable path toward spatially competent VLMs.

## 5. The Benchmarking Explosion: Systematic Evaluation of Spatial Reasoning

The rapid growth of spatial models has been accompanied by a proliferation of rigorous benchmarks that isolate and measure spatial intelligence. The What'sUp benchmark (Kamath et al., 2023) [1] at EMNLP 2023 set the template by precisely controlling for confounds. GSR-Bench (Rajabi & Kosecka, 2024) [13] at the NeurIPS 2024 Workshop extended this line by enriching What'sUp with additional supervision and evaluating 27 models across three classes of MLLMs, revealing scaling laws where larger models show only marginal improvements.

BLINK (Fu et al., 2024) [14] at ECCV 2024 cast a wider net by reformulating 14 classic computer vision tasks—including relative depth estimation, visual correspondence, forensics detection, and multi-view reasoning—into 3,807 multiple-choice questions solvable by humans "within a blink" (95.70% accuracy). The best-performing model, GPT-4V, achieved only 51.26%, exposing a vast chasm between human perception and VLMs. EmbSpatial-Bench (Chen et al., 2024) [15] at ACL 2024 (Short) specifically targets embodied spatial understanding for robotics and navigation tasks, evaluating how well VLMs comprehend spatial layouts in egocentric views. 3DSRBench (Ma et al., 2025) [16] at ICCV 2025 provides the first comprehensive 3D spatial reasoning benchmark, featuring 3,000 annotated image-question-answer triplets across 12 question types. By including paired images from common and uncommon viewpoints and adopting a novel FlipEval protocol, 3DSRBench reveals that models degrade significantly on rare viewpoints, and that even strong models struggle with height, orientation, and multi-object reasoning.

The Cambrian-1 project (Tong et al., 2024) [17] at NeurIPS 2024 (Oral) offered a systematic, vision-centric exploration of MLLM design. Beyond proposing the Spatial Vision Aggregator (SVA)—a dynamic, spatially-aware connector that integrates features from multiple vision encoders with explicit spatial inductive bias—the authors introduced CV-Bench, a vision-centric benchmark, and performed rigorous experiments with over 15 vision encoders. Their findings show that spatial reasoning benefits from combining self-supervised and strongly supervised visual representations, and that the SVA's architecture, which performs vision feature aggregation across multiple LLM layers, significantly improves grounded reasoning.

## 6. Emerging Directions: Quantitative Estimation, Embodied Reasoning, and Cross-Cultural Perspectives

Two recent works at EMNLP 2024 point toward emerging frontiers. Liao et al. (2024) [18] showed that reasoning paths with reference objects can elicit quantitative spatial reasoning (object sizes and distances) in large VLMs, demonstrating that structured prompting with explicit reference frames enables models to output metric estimates. Mirzaee & Kordjamshidi (2023) [19] at EMNLP 2023 (Findings) tackled the disentanglement of extraction and reasoning in multi-hop spatial reasoning, isolating the linguistic and visual components of the problem. Finally, the COMFORT protocol (Zhang et al., 2024) [20] at the NeurIPS 2024 Pluralistic Alignment Workshop introduced a cross-cultural perspective, evaluating VLMs on spatial frame-of-reference ambiguities across languages. They found that VLMs align with English conventions but lack robustness, flexibility across coordinate systems, and cross-cultural adherence—a critical reminder that spatial reasoning is not a universal capability but one shaped by linguistic and cultural conventions.

## 7. Summary

The 2022–2025 period has witnessed a dramatic transformation in spatial understanding for VLMs, moving from the initial diagnosis of near-chance performance on basic spatial relations to the development of specialized architectures (SpatialRGPT, 3D-LLM, Cambrian-1), scalable data solutions (SpatialVLM), and comprehensive benchmarks (SpatialEval, BLINK, 3DSRBench). Key findings include: (i) spatial reasoning deficits are deeply rooted in training data and architecture, not merely scale; (ii) depth-aware connectors and 3D data synthesis are effective remedies; (iii) code-generation and programmatic decomposition offer interpretable alternatives; and (iv) evaluation must control for confounds like viewpoint rarity and cultural bias. Despite significant progress, the gap between human and machine spatial intelligence remains substantial, particularly in metric estimation, rare viewpoints, and cross-cultural generalization.
