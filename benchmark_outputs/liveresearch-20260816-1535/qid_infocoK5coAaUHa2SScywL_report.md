

# AI Portfolio-Management & Financial-Planning “Copilots”: U.S. & Canadian Bank / Broker-Dealer Launches (2023–2026)

## Due-Diligence Research Report

*Date of research: August 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

Between 2023 and mid‑2026, a wave of production‑grade, client‑facing AI assistants has reshaped wealth management in North America. U.S. and Canadian banks and broker‑dealers have deployed generative‑AI co‑pilots that directly serve retail and mass‑affluent investors (portfolio insights, conversational wealth management, thematic investing) or that equip financial advisors with client‑facing tools that generate client‑ready output. The table below captures the institutions, product names, launch dates, technology stacks, compliance postures, integrations, adoption metrics, and source links for the most significant launches.

**Scope note:** This report covers **client‑facing AI portfolio‑management or financial‑planning “copilots”** — i.e., tools that are used directly by end clients or that produce client‑ready output (such as portfolio narratives, meeting summaries, or investment themes). Internal‑only advisor tools are included only when they have a direct client‑facing component (e.g., AI‑generated client summaries). The cut‑off date is August 2026.

## Key Observations

### 1. The Inflection Point: June 2026
Multiple sources identify June 2026 as a watershed month. Schwab (retail portfolio insights), Morgan Stanley (external AI agent connectivity), and Citi (Citi Sky) all launched or announced client‑facing AI capabilities in the same period. According to BCG data cited by InvestSuite, early‑mover firms see 15–20% revenue‑per‑advisor uplifts.

### 2. Client‑Facing vs. Advisor‑Facing Spectrum
Few tools are purely client‑facing self‑service. The dominant model is a **hybrid “copilot” that generates client‑ready output** (summaries, insights, meeting notes) that the advisor then reviews and delivers. Only **Schwab’s Portfolio Insights**, **Citi Sky**, **Wells Fargo’s Fargo**, and **JPMorgan’s IndexGPT** are directly used by clients without mandatory advisor intermediation. Most other tools are “client‑adjacent” — they improve the client experience via advisor‑mediated AI.

### 3. AI Stack Patterns
- **OpenAI GPT‑4** dominates the U.S. wirehouses (Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan for IndexGPT).
- **Google Cloud / DeepMind** powers Citi Sky and Wells Fargo’s Fargo.
- **Proprietary / in‑house LLMs** are used by RBC (Borealis AI), CIBC, and UBS (STAAT).
- **Microsoft Copilot** is widely deployed for general productivity (UBS 50k licenses, BMO enterprise‑wide).
- **Anthropic** (Claude) is used by RBC for Household Summary Meeting Preparation.

### 4. Safety & Compliance
All institutions have implemented:
- **Consent mechanisms** for AI meeting recording.
- **Human‑in‑the‑loop** review before client‑facing output.
- **Audit logging** of all AI interactions.
- **SOC2 / bank‑level security** environments.
- **Disclaimers** that AI output is not investment advice.
- **Model governance** frameworks (e.g., Morgan Stanley’s eval framework, RBC’s Responsible AI).

### 5. Integration Ecosystem
The most common integrations are:
- **CRM**: Salesforce (dominant), Microsoft Dynamics.
- **Meeting platforms**: Zoom, Teams.
- **Portfolio accounting**: Proprietary platforms (Vanguard, Schwab, Fidelity), Addepar, Orion.
- **Productivity suites**: Microsoft 365, Google Workspace.
- **SSO & audit**: All major banks use enterprise SSO and full audit trails.

### 6. Pricing Model
Every tool listed is **free to the advisor or client** as part of the existing brokerage or banking relationship. No institution has yet introduced a separate fee for AI copilot capabilities.

### 7. KPI Landscape
- **Adoption**: 98% (Morgan Stanley Assistant), 90% (UBS STAAT, Charles Schwab Knowledge Assistant).
- **Time savings**: 30–40 min per meeting (RBC, CIBC), up to 4 hours per meeting (BofA), 1,200 hours/week (UBS).
- **ROI**: $170M in AI‑driven value in 2025 (TD Bank target); $10M+ annual labor cost reduction (Schwab).
- **Client satisfaction**: Not yet widely published, but Morgan Stanley reports positive client feedback on faster follow‑ups.
