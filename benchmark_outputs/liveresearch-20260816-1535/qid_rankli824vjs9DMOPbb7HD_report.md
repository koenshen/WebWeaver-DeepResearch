

# Top 12 Gaming Monitors of 2026: A Ranked Analysis by Refresh Rate, Response Time, and Price per Inch

## Methodology

The following ranking evaluates gaming monitors available or released for purchase in 2026 using three equally weighted criteria:

- **Refresh Rate (Hz)** – higher is better
- **Response Time (ms)** – lower is better
- **Price per Inch of Screen Size** – lower is better

Each metric was normalized to a 0–1 scale using the following reference maxima: 600 Hz for refresh rate, 1.0 ms for response time, and $60 per inch for price-per-inch. The composite score is the arithmetic mean of the three normalized sub-scores. All prices are street prices (USD) as of mid-2026. The list includes both OLED and LCD panels across multiple sizes and resolutions.

## Analysis

### Tier 1: Ultra-High Refresh Rate OLEDs (Ranks 1–6)

The top six positions are dominated by 27-inch 1440p OLED monitors with refresh rates at or above 480 Hz and response times of 0.02–0.03 ms. These models represent the cutting edge of gaming display technology in 2026, where OLED pixel response has effectively neutralised the motion-blur advantage that TN panels once held at extreme refresh rates.

**1st place: MSI MAG 272QP QD-OLED X50 (Score: 0.790)**  
This monitor achieves the highest composite score by combining a 500 Hz refresh rate and 0.03 ms response time with a relatively accessible price of $699. At $25.89 per inch, it offers the best price-per-inch among the 500 Hz OLED class, making it the most balanced performer across all three criteria. The 3rd-generation QD-OLED panel delivers excellent colour volume and HDR performance, while the inclusion of DisplayPort 2.1a, USB-C with 98 W power delivery, and a three-year burn-in warranty adds substantial value. Its closest competitor, the MSI MPG 271QR X50, costs $100 more for essentially the same core panel, which pushes it to third place.

**2nd place: ASUS ROG Swift OLED PG27AQDP (Score: 0.784)**  
At 480 Hz, this monitor trades 20 Hz of peak refresh rate versus the MSI MAG 272QP but compensates with a marginally lower price ($679) and the same 0.03 ms response time. Its price-per-inch of $25.15 is the best among all monitors ranked 1–6. The WOLED panel provides exceptional contrast and near-instantaneous pixel transitions. The 480 Hz ceiling is more than sufficient for all but the most extreme esports scenarios, and the monitor's ELMB motion-blur reduction further enhances clarity.

**4th place: LG UltraGear OLED GX7 (27GX790B-B) (Score: 0.754)**  
The LG GX7 is the fastest OLED monitor on this list by raw refresh rate, offering 540 Hz at native 1440p and a dual-mode 720 Hz at 720p. Its 0.02 ms response time is the joint-fastest available. However, its $999 price tag yields a price-per-inch of $37.00, which penalises its composite score relative to the lower-priced 500 Hz models. This monitor is built for competitive gamers who prioritise maximum motion clarity and are willing to pay a premium. It also features a 4th-generation WOLED panel with improved brightness and a free 240 Hz secondary monitor during pre-order periods.

**5th place: ASUS ROG Swift OLED PG27AQWP-W (Score: 0.734)**  
The PG27AQWP-W is essentially the 540 Hz counterpart to the 480 Hz PG27AQDP, sharing the same 0.02 ms response time and 4th-gen WOLED technology. Its $1,099 price raises the price-per-inch to $40.70, the highest among the 27-inch OLEDs, which explains its lower rank despite the higher refresh rate. The premium buys a dual-mode capability (720 Hz at 720p) and a glossy TrueBlack coating that improves perceived contrast in controlled lighting, but for most users the PG27AQDP offers better value.

**6th place: Samsung Odyssey OLED G6 G60SF (Score: 0.729)**  
Samsung's 500 Hz QD-OLED offering matches the MSI models in refresh rate and response time but carries a $999 price, giving it the same price-per-inch penalty as the LG GX7. The G60SF features Samsung's Glare-Free coating and a thermal modulation system for burn-in prevention, but the $300 premium over the MSI MAG 272QP is difficult to justify on objective metrics alone. It remains a strong choice for users invested in the Samsung ecosystem.

### Tier 2: High-Value 360 Hz OLED (Rank 7)

**7th place: Dell Alienware AW2725DF (Score: 0.723)**  
This monitor is the standout value proposition in the 360 Hz OLED segment. At $649 and $24.04 per inch, it undercuts the 500 Hz OLEDs by $150–$450 while delivering the same 0.03 ms response time. The 360 Hz refresh rate is widely regarded as the practical threshold where motion clarity matches 540 Hz TN panels in real-world gaming, thanks to OLED's superior pixel response. The AW2725DF includes a three-year burn-in warranty and active cooling, making it a reliable choice for extended gaming sessions. Its composite score is only 0.031 points behind the top-ranked MSI MAG 272QP, illustrating that the refresh-rate premium for 500 Hz+ comes at a significant cost.

### Tier 3: Esports Specialists (Ranks 8–9)

**8th place: ASUS ROG Strix OLED XG259QWPG Ace (Score: 0.711)**  
As the world's first 24.5-inch OLED esports monitor, the XG259QWPG Ace brings 540 Hz and 0.02 ms response time to a smaller form factor preferred by many competitive players. However, its 1080p resolution and estimated $1,099 price yield a very high price-per-inch of $44.86, which drags down its composite score. The monitor's Tandem WOLED panel offers 15% higher peak brightness and 60% longer lifespan than previous WOLED generations, but the value proposition is narrow: it is optimised for professional esports where size and speed trump resolution and cost.

**9th place: ASUS ROG Swift Pro PG248QP (Score: 0.696)**  
This 540 Hz E-TN (esports TN) panel targets the same audience as the XG259QWPG Ace but at a lower price ($899, $36.69/in) and with a slower 0.2 ms response time. Its principal advantage is ULMB 2 backlight strobing, which can push effective motion clarity beyond what 540 Hz alone provides. However, the TN panel's colour reproduction and viewing angles are significantly inferior to OLED alternatives, and the composite score reflects this trade-off. For professional CS2 and Valorant players who prioritise absolute motion clarity over image quality, this remains a valid choice.

### Tier 4: Mid-Range OLED Value (Ranks 10–11)

**10th place: Gigabyte MO27Q28G (Score: 0.689)**  
This 280 Hz WOLED monitor is the best value OLED on the list, with a price-per-inch of just $22.19. Its 0.03 ms response time matches the flagship models, but the lower refresh rate limits its composite score. The MO27Q28G is an excellent choice for gamers who want OLED quality—deep blacks, vibrant colours, and near-instantaneous response—without paying the premium for 480 Hz+. It also includes a KVM switch and USB-C connectivity, making it suitable for mixed productivity and gaming use.

**11th place: ASUS ROG Strix OLED XG27AQDMG (Score: 0.677)**  
At 240 Hz and $549, this monitor offers the lowest price-per-inch among all OLEDs on the list ($20.33). It shares the 0.03 ms response time of higher-tier models, making it an excellent entry point into OLED gaming. The 240 Hz refresh rate is sufficient for the vast majority of gamers, and the glossy WOLED panel provides superb image quality. Its composite score is held back by the 240 Hz ceiling, but for value-conscious buyers, this may be the most compelling monitor on the list.

### Tier 5: High-Refresh IPS (Rank 12)

**12th place: Dell Alienware AW2524HF (Score: 0.664)**  
The only non-OLED monitor in the top 12, the AW2524HF achieves 500 Hz on a Fast IPS panel with a 0.5 ms response time. Its price of $499 and $20.37 per inch are among the lowest on the list, but the slower response time (0.5 ms vs. 0.02–0.03 ms for OLEDs) significantly reduces its composite score. While IPS panels have improved dramatically, they cannot match the instantaneous pixel transitions of OLED. This monitor is a strong choice for budget-conscious competitive gamers who need 500 Hz but cannot afford OLED pricing.

## Key Observations

**1. OLED dominates the top 11 positions.** The combination of 0.02–0.03 ms response times and high refresh rates (240–540 Hz) makes OLED the undisputed leader in gaming monitor performance. The only LCD in the top 12 (Alienware AW2524HF) ranks last due to its slower response time.

**2. The refresh-rate ceiling is rising rapidly.** In 2026, 500 Hz is no longer a niche esports specification; five monitors in the top 12 offer 500 Hz or higher, and three offer 540 Hz. The Samsung Odyssey G6 G60H (1,040 Hz at 720p) was announced but not yet widely available at the time of analysis; it would likely rank highly once pricing is confirmed.

**3. Price-per-inch is the primary differentiator.** Among monitors with similar refresh rates and response times (e.g., 500 Hz/0.03 ms), the price-per-inch metric determines the ranking. The MSI MAG 272QP X50 ($25.89/in) outranks the Samsung G60SF ($37.00/in) and ASUS PG27AQWP-W ($40.70/in) despite identical core specifications.

**4. 360 Hz OLED offers the best balance of performance and value.** The Dell Alienware AW2725DF (360 Hz, $649) scores 0.723—only 0.067 points behind the top-ranked 500 Hz model—demonstrating that the incremental motion-clarity benefit of 500 Hz over 360 Hz is modest in practice, especially given OLED's superior pixel response.

**5. Size matters in the price-per-inch calculation.** The two 24.5-inch monitors (XG259QWPG Ace and PG248QP) are penalised by their smaller screens, which inflate their price-per-inch figures. This does not necessarily reflect poor value, as the 24.5-inch form factor is preferred by some esports players for its lower eye movement requirements.
