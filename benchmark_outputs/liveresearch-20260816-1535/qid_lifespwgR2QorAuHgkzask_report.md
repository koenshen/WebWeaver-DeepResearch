
# Comparative Effectiveness of HIIT, MICT, and Hybrid Protocols in Adults Aged 30–70 with Limited Training Time: A Comprehensive Research Report

## 1. Definitions and Protocol Characterisation

### 1.1 High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT)
HIIT is defined as intermittent exercise involving alternating bouts of vigorous- or near-maximal-intensity exercise interspersed with lower-intensity recovery periods. The ACSM operationalises HIIT as work intervals performed at ≥80% of maximal heart rate (HRmax) or ≥85% of maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max), with recovery periods allowing partial but incomplete restoration of heart rate (ACSM, 2022 position stand). The 2018 Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans (2nd edition) similarly describes HIIT as alternating short periods of maximal-effort exercise with less intense recovery, noting no universally accepted interval lengths, ratios, or session volumes.

**Key sub-types:**
- **Classic HIIT (high-volume):** 4 × 4 min at 85–95% HRmax with 3 min active recovery at 60–70% HRmax (the "Norwegian 4×4" protocol). Session time: ~38 min; exercise time: ~16 min.
- **Low-volume HIIT (LV-HIIT):** Protocols with ≤15 min of high-intensity work per session. Typical example: 10 × 1 min at 85–90% HRmax with 1 min recovery. Session time: ~20 min.
- **Sprint Interval Training (SIT):** "All-out" or supramaximal efforts (>100% maximal work rate), typically 4–6 × 30‑s Wingate sprints with 4–4.5 min recovery. Session time: ~20–25 min; total intense exercise: 2–3 min.
- **Reduced-Exertion HIIT (REHIT):** A low-dose SIT variant, e.g., 2 × 20‑s all-out sprints within a 10‑min session, designed for sedentary or clinical populations.

### 1.2 Moderate-Intensity Continuous Training (MICT)
Continuous aerobic exercise at 55–70% HRmax or 40–60% of heart rate reserve (HRR), typically 30–60 min per session, at an intensity corresponding to a rating of perceived exertion (RPE) of 11–13 on the Borg 6–20 scale. The WHO/ACSM minimum prescription is 150 min/week of moderate-intensity activity.

### 1.3 Hybrid HIIT+MICT Protocols
Protocols that combine both HIIT and MICT elements within a single session or across a weekly microcycle. Examples include:
- **Polarised training:** ~80% of weekly volume at low-to-moderate intensity (MICT) and ~20% at high intensity (HIIT).
- **Combined session protocols:** For example, 20 min MICT (warm-up) followed by 3–4 × 3 min HIIT intervals, then cool-down.
- **Alternating-day protocols:** HIIT on 2 days/week and MICT on 1–2 days/week.

## 2. Cardiorespiratory Fitness (CRF): VO₂max/VO₂peak

### 2.1 HIIT vs. MICT
A large body of meta-analytic evidence from 2018–2026 consistently demonstrates that **HIIT produces superior or equivalent improvements in CRF compared to MICT, with a significantly lower time commitment**.

- **Younger and middle-aged adults (18–65 years):** A 2023 meta-analysis of 29 RCTs (n = 807) found that HIIT significantly improved VO₂peak relative to MICT (SMD = 0.19, 95% CI 0.03–0.34, p = 0.021), with a between-group difference favouring HIIT of approximately 1–2 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (PMC10048683, 2023). A 2026 network meta-analysis (NMA) of 18 RCTs in overweight/obese adults found that HIIT ≥75 min/week produced the greatest improvement in VO₂max (MD = 5.94 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹, 95% CrI 2.97–8.88) compared to control, ranking first by SUCRA (88.68%) (Deng et al., *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil*, 2026).

- **Older adults (≥60 years):** A 2026 meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (n = 1,434) in healthy older adults reported that HIIT improved VO₂max by MD = 1.62 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (95% CI 1.10–2.13) and MICT by MD = 1.22 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (95% CI 0.90–1.53); in direct comparisons, HIIT was superior (MD = 1.17, 95% CI 0.52–1.82, p < 0.01) (Chu et al., *Medicine*, 2026).

- **Very low-volume HIIT:** A 2024 meta-analysis of LV-HIIT (≤15 min intense exercise per session) found that LV-HIIT was at least equivalent to MICT for improving CRF, despite requiring only 14–47% of the exercise time commitment (cdnsciencepub.com, 2024). Gillen et al. (2016, *PLoS ONE*) demonstrated that SIT (1 min of intense exercise within a 10‑min session, 3 days/week) produced a ~6 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ increase in VO₂peak over 12 weeks, identical to MICT requiring 50 min/session.

### 2.2 Hybrid HIIT+MICT Protocols
Evidence for hybrid protocols is more limited. The 2026 NMA (Deng et al.) examined HIIT with recovery modulation (a form of hybrid) and found it ranked second for VO₂max improvement (SUCRA ~ 80%), below HIIT ≥75 min/week but above MICT. The Stensvold et al. (2020) Generation 100 study (5‑year follow-up in older adults) compared twice-weekly HIIT (4 × 4 min) vs. twice-weekly MICT (50 min) vs. physical activity recommendations; HIIT produced the greatest improvements in CRF and survival, with MICT intermediate.

### 2.3 Minimal Effective Dose for CRF Enhancement
- **HIIT:** ≥75 min/week of vigorous activity (equivalent to ≥2 sessions/week of 20 min HIIT) appears to be the minimal effective dose for significant CRF improvement in adults with limited time (Deng et al., 2026).
- **SIT/LV-HIIT:** As little as 3 min/week of all-out sprinting (e.g., 3 × 20‑s sprints, 3 days/week) can improve VO₂peak, though the magnitude of effect is dose-dependent.
- **MICT:** The standard 150 min/week threshold is well-established; however, doses as low as 60 min/week (2 × 30 min) produce measurable but smaller improvements (WHO 2020 guidelines).

## 3. Cardiometabolic Outcomes

### 3.1 Glycaemic Control (HbA1c, Fasting Glucose, HOMA-IR)

**HIIT vs. MICT:**
- A 2024 meta-analysis of 30 RCTs in type 2 diabetes (T2D) patients found that HIIT significantly reduced HbA1c (MD = −0.60%, 95% CI −0.84 to −0.36, p < 0.01), with larger effect sizes for interventions ≤8 weeks (Feng et al., *Front Endocrinol*, 2024).
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 14 RCTs in diabesity (T2D + obesity) found HIIT significantly reduced fasting glucose (SMD −0.64 mmol/L, 95% CI −1.03 to −0.24), HbA1c (SMD −1.08%, 95% CI −1.68 to −0.47), fasting insulin (SMD −0.79 mIU/L, 95% CI −1.28 to −0.31), and HOMA-IR (SMD −1.13, 95% CI −2.15 to −0.12) compared to standard care (Demir et al., *Diabetol Metab Syndr*, 2025). However, certainty of evidence was rated as very low due to high heterogeneity.
- In head-to-head comparisons with MICT, HIIT has shown similar or slightly superior glycaemic improvements, though the difference is often not statistically significant. A 2021 meta-analysis (Poon et al., *J Sports Sci*) of middle-aged and older adults found no significant between-group difference in HbA1c reduction.
- **Time-efficient protocols:** The 12‑week SIT protocol (3 min/week total sprint time) by Gillen et al. (2016) produced a 31% reduction in fasting insulin (vs. 9% for MICT), with a 24% reduction in HOMA-IR, suggesting that even minimal volumes of high-intensity exercise can meaningfully improve insulin sensitivity.

**Hybrid protocols:** No dedicated meta-analyses were identified for hybrid HIIT+MICT protocols on glycaemic outcomes. However, the 2024 meta-analysis of combined exercise (aerobic + resistance) in sedentary adults found significant reductions in fasting glucose (SMD −0.47), fasting insulin (SMD −1.02), and HOMA-IR (SMD −0.95), but no significant effect on HbA1c (Nature, *Scientific Reports*, 2024).

### 3.2 Blood Pressure

**HIIT vs. MICT:**
- A 2022 meta-analysis of 13 RCTs (n = 442) in hypertensive patients found no significant difference between HIIT and MICT in reducing resting SBP (WMD −1.09 mmHg, 95% CI −3.19 to 1.02) or DBP (WMD −0.67 mmHg, 95% CI −1.95 to 0.62) (PMC9771301, 2022). However, **subgroup analyses revealed that HIIT was superior to MICT in reducing daytime ambulatory SBP** (WMD −4.14 mmHg, 95% CI −6.98 to −1.30) and evening SBP (WMD −10.26 mmHg, 95% CI −12.65 to −7.87).
- A 2024 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs found HIIT produced small reductions in SBP (MD ≈ −2 mmHg) with minimal impact on DBP, concluding the clinical relevance was limited (*Life*, 2024).
- In the 2023 meta-analysis of 29 RCTs (young/middle-aged, overweight/obese), both HIIT and MICT significantly reduced SBP (HIIT: MD −3.83 mmHg; MICT: MD −3.56 mmHg) and DBP (HIIT: MD −1.59 mmHg; MICT: MD −1.88 mmHg), with no statistically significant between-group differences (PMC10048683, 2023).

**Hybrid protocols:** A 2026 meta-analysis of combined exercise (aerobic + resistance) found significant reductions in both SBP and DBP, with combined training showing the largest effects among all exercise modalities (ScienceDirect, 2026).

### 3.3 Lipid Profile

**HIIT vs. MICT:**
- A 2023 meta-analysis of 8 RCTs in nondiabetic overweight/obese young adults found that HIIT significantly decreased LDL‑C (MD −6.23 mg/dL, p = 0.05) and total cholesterol (MD −7.85 mg/dL, p = 0.02) compared to MICT, with no significant differences in HDL‑C or triglycerides (PMC10464775, 2023).
- In the 2023 meta-analysis of 29 RCTs, no significant between-group differences were found for total cholesterol, LDL‑C, HDL‑C, or triglycerides between HIIT and MICT in young/middle-aged adults (PMC10048683, 2023).
- A 2021 review of HIIT for NAFLD found that HIIT significantly reduced triglycerides and improved HDL‑C similarly to MICT, despite requiring less time and energy expenditure (Sabag et al., Western Sydney University, 2021).

**NAFLD-specific outcomes:**
- A 2024 network meta-analysis of 42 studies (n = 2,070) in NAFLD patients found that combined aerobic + resistance training (AT + RT) was best for TC (SUCRA = 71.7) and TG (SUCRA = 96.8), while HIIT ranked second for TC (SUCRA = 69.0), TG (SUCRA = 69.1), and LDL‑C (SUCRA = 84.2). Aerobic training (MICT) was best for HDL‑C (SUCRA = 72.3) (Sci Rep, 2024).

### 3.4 Adiposity and Liver Fat

**Body composition:**
- The 2023 meta-analysis of 29 RCTs found HIIT significantly superior to MICT for reducing waist circumference (MD −0.96 cm, p = 0.037) and percent fat mass (MD −0.48%, p = 0.014), but not body mass, BMI, or fat-free mass (PMC10048683, 2023).
- The 2026 NMA in overweight/obese adults found HIIT ≥75 min/week ranked first for reducing body fat percentage (MD −3.05%, 95% CrI −4.69 to −1.42), waist-to-hip ratio (MD −0.05, 95% CrI −0.08 to −0.02), and waist circumference (MD −4.82 cm, 95% CrI −9.21 to −0.46) (Deng et al., 2026).

**Liver fat:**
- A 2015 landmark RCT (n = 23, NAFLD patients) showed that 12 weeks of modified HIIT (3 × week, 30–40 min) reduced intrahepatic lipid from 11% to 8% (p = 0.019), compared to no change in controls (Houghton et al., *J Physiol*, 2015).
- A 2021 systematic review confirmed that HIIT elicits comparable improvements in liver fat to MICT despite requiring less energy and time commitment (Sabag et al., *J Clin Endocrinol Metab*, 2021).
- A 2023 Penn State meta-analysis concluded that ≥150 min/week of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic activity (including HIIT or MICT) achieves clinically meaningful reductions in liver fat for NAFLD patients (*Penn State Health News*, 2023).
- **Minimal effective dose for liver fat:** The 2023 Penn State study identified 150 min/week of moderate-to-vigorous activity as the threshold for significant liver fat reduction; HIIT can achieve this with ~75 min/week of vigorous activity.

## 4. Patient-Centered Outcomes

### 4.1 Adherence and Compliance

**Supervised settings:**
- A landmark 2023 meta-analysis of 188 studies (n = 8,928) reported that compliance to supervised HIIT averaged 89.4% (SD 11.8%) and to supervised MICT averaged 92.5% (SD 10.6%), with **no statistically significant difference between modalities** (Hedge's g = 0.015, 95% CI −0.088 to 0.118, p = 0.78) (Santos et al., *Int J Behav Nutr Phys Act*, 2023; PMC10664287).

**Unsupervised/real-world settings:**
- Adherence to unsupervised HIIT averaged 63% (SD 21.1%) and to unsupervised MICT 68.2% (SD 16.2%). The between-group difference was not statistically significant (Hedge's g = −0.313, 95% CI −0.681 to 0.056, p = 0.096), though the evidence was rated as very low quality (Santos et al., 2023).
- Subgroup analysis revealed that the method of measurement mattered: **activity trackers favoured MICT adherence**, while self-report measures showed similar adherence between HIIT and MICT.
- In the 2023 meta-analysis of 29 RCTs, the mean dropout rate was 9.76% for HIIT vs. 7.72% for MICT (PMC10048683, 2023).

### 4.2 Enjoyment

- A 2024 meta-analysis of 16 studies (n = 537) in overweight/obese adults found that **HIIT elicited significantly higher enjoyment than MICT** (SMD = 0.47, 95% CI 0.12–0.81, p < 0.05), particularly in acute/single-session studies, while affective responses (Feeling Scale) and arousal (Felt Arousal Scale) were similar between modalities (Frontiers in Public Health, 2024).
- A 2016 study (Martinez et al., *PLoS ONE*) demonstrated that HIIT (30- and 60-s intervals) was rated as more enjoyable than MICT, despite higher RPE, heart rate, and blood lactate, attributed to the time efficiency and varied stimulus.
- The 2023 meta-analysis also noted that HIIT appears to be more enjoyable than MICT, and this may contribute to better long-term adherence despite higher perceived exertion (PMC10048683, 2023).
- **Interval duration matters:** Intervals ≤60 s with a 1:1 work-to-rest ratio are associated with the most favourable enjoyment and affective responses (Martinez et al., 2016).

### 4.3 Adverse Events

- The 2023 meta-analysis of 188 studies found that **adverse event rates were low and not significantly different between HIIT and MICT** in supervised settings (Santos et al., 2023).
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 18 RCTs in older adults found no significant difference in adverse events between HIIT and other exercises (RR = 1.34, 95% CI 0.92–1.95, p = 0.13, I² = 0%) (ScienceDirect, 2025).
- In hypertensive patients, a 2022 meta-analysis confirmed no significant difference in adverse events between HIIT and MICT (PMC9771301, 2022).
- **Safety considerations:** The ACSM and WHO guidelines highlight that the benefits of physical activity outweigh the risks, and that gradual progression can mitigate potential harms. A 2020 review of home-based HIIT found it as safe as MICT for cardiac rehabilitation patients (PMC10696712, 2023).

## 5. Guidelines and Recommendations

### 5.1 World Health Organization (WHO) 2020 Guidelines
- **Core recommendation:** Adults should undertake 150–300 min/week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75–150 min/week of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity, or an equivalent combination.
- **HIIT applicability:** The 75 min/week threshold for vigorous activity directly aligns with HIIT protocols (e.g., 3 × 25 min sessions/week).
- **Bout duration:** Any bout duration now counts; there is no longer a 10‑minute minimum, which supports the validity of short HIIT sessions.
- **Dose-response:** Greater volumes (up to 300 min moderate or 150 min vigorous) provide additional health benefits.
- **Muscle strengthening:** 2+ days/week of muscle-strengthening activities are recommended in addition to aerobic exercise.
- **Sedentary behaviour:** Limiting sedentary time is emphasised, with a recommendation to replace sedentary time with any intensity of activity.

### 5.2 ACSM Guidelines (2022–2025)
- **Core recommendation:** 30 min of moderate-intensity aerobic activity on ≥5 days/week, or 20 min of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity on ≥3 days/week, plus 2 days/week of resistance training.
- **HIIT position stand:** The ACSM has a dedicated position stand on "High-Intensity Interval Training for Cardiometabolic Disease Prevention" (MSSE, 2019), which endorses HIIT as an effective and time-efficient alternative to MICT.
- **2025 guidelines:** The 2025 ACSM guidelines emphasise a well-rounded approach including body composition, flexibility, muscular strength, and cardiorespiratory endurance, with HIIT explicitly recognised as a time-efficient strategy.
- **HIIT prescription parameters (ACSM):** Intensity ≥80% HRmax; work intervals 1–4 min; work-to-rest ratio 1:1 to 1:2; frequency 2–3 days/week; session duration 20–45 min.

### 5.3 ACSM Resistance Training Position Stand (2026)
- The 2026 updated position stand highlights that "minimal doses" of resistance training can produce substantial strength and health gains, and that individualisation is more important than rigid criteria. This is relevant for combined HIIT+RT protocols.

### 5.4 Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans (2nd Edition, 2018)
- Explicitly discusses HIIT as a form of interval training that can improve insulin sensitivity, blood pressure, and body composition, with greater benefits in overweight/obese and high-risk adults.
- Notes that HIIT may provide similar reductions in CVD risk factors as continuous moderate-intensity activity.

## 6. Progression and Maintenance Plans

### 6.1 Session Frequency and Intensity Prescription

| Parameter | HIIT (General) | LV-HIIT/SIT | MICT | Hybrid |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|------|--------|
| **Frequency** | 2–3 days/week | 2–3 days/week | 3–5 days/week | 2–3 days/week (HIIT) + 1–2 days/week (MICT) |
| **Intensity (work intervals)** | 80–95% HRmax or ≥85% VO₂max | "All-out" (supramaximal, >100% maximal work rate) | 55–70% HRmax or 40–60% HRR | 80–95% HRmax (HIIT components) + 55–70% HRmax (MICT components) |
| **Interval duration** | 1–4 min | 20–30 s | N/A | 1–4 min (HIIT) + 20–40 min (MICT) |
| **Work-to-rest ratio** | 1:1 to 1:2 | 1:4 to 1:8 | N/A | Variable |
| **Session duration** | 20–45 min | 10–25 min | 30–60 min | 30–50 min |
| **Weekly vigorous volume** | ≥75 min | 10–30 min | N/A | ≥75 min (HIIT portion) |

### 6.2 Minimal Effective Dose (for <150 min/week and <60 min/week subgroups)

**For adults with <150 min/week available:**
- **HIIT:** 2 sessions/week × 20–25 min (total 50 min/week) of 4 × 4 min or 10 × 1 min protocols can produce significant improvements in VO₂max, insulin sensitivity, and blood pressure.
- **SIT:** 2–3 sessions/week × 10–15 min (total 20–45 min/week) of 4–6 × 30‑s sprints is sufficient for CRF and metabolic improvements.
- **MICT:** 3 sessions/week × 30 min (total 90 min/week) at 60–70% HRmax can produce meaningful CRF improvements, though less than standard 150 min/week.

**For adults with <60 min/week available (the "ultra-time-efficient" subgroup):**
- **SIT/LV-HIIT:** 3 sessions/week × 10 min (total 30 min/week) of 3–5 × 30‑s sprints or 2 × 20‑s REHIT protocol can improve VO₂peak by ~3–6 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ and reduce insulin resistance.
- **HIIT (modified):** 2 sessions/week × 20 min (total 40 min/week) of 1 × 4 min + 3 × 1 min intermittent intervals.
- **MICT:** At <60 min/week, MICT (e.g., 2 × 30 min) is unlikely to meet the 150 min/week threshold, yielding suboptimal CRF and metabolic benefits. However, any activity is better than none.

**Key evidence for ultra-low volume:**
- Gillen et al. (2016, *PLoS ONE*): 3 min/week of sprinting (3 × 20‑s sprints, 3 days/week) improved VO₂peak by ~6 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹—equivalent to 150 min/week of MICT.
- Gibala et al. (2012, *J Physiol*): 2.5 h/week of low-volume HIIT produced similar improvements in muscle oxidative capacity and insulin sensitivity as 10.5 h/week of MICT.
- The 2026 NMA (Deng et al.) found that even HIIT <75 min/week (approximately 40–60 min/week) improved body fat percentage (MD −2.53%) and other outcomes, though less than HIIT ≥75 min/week.

### 6.3 Supervised vs. Home-Based Delivery

**Supervised HIIT:**
- Compliance rates are high (~89%) and comparable to supervised MICT.
- The 2023 meta-analysis confirmed that supervised HIIT and MICT have similar compliance rates (Hedge's g = 0.015, p = 0.78).
- Essential for initial sessions in high-risk populations (e.g., hypertension, T2D, NAFLD, older adults ≥60).

**Home-based HIIT:**
- A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis (PMC10696712, 2023) found that **home-based HIIT significantly improves CRF** (SMD 0.43, 95% CI 0.23–0.62) and is as safe as MICT for cardiac rehabilitation patients.
- Adherence to unsupervised HIIT is moderate (~63%) and lower than to supervised HIIT (~89%), but comparable to unsupervised MICT (~68%) (Santos et al., 2023).
- **Technology-assisted delivery:** Smartphone apps, online videos, and wearable heart rate monitors can improve adherence to home-based HIIT. Self-paced protocols (e.g., "talk test" or RPE-based) are feasible alternatives to heart rate monitors.
- **Long-term maintenance:** A 2‑year follow-up of the TREHIIT trial (Nature, 2025) found that women with overweight/obesity who were prescribed 3 × week HIIT reported a decline from 1.9 to 1.0 HIIT sessions/week after 12 months, with a concomitant increase in moderate-intensity activity. This suggests that **long-term adherence to unsupervised HIIT declines, and many individuals spontaneously transition to MICT.**

### 6.4 Progression Models for Sustaining Gains Over ≥6–12 Months

**Progression should follow the FITT-VP principle (Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type, Volume, Pattern, Progression):**

**Phase 1 (Weeks 1–4): Initial adaptation**
- HIIT: 2 sessions/week, 4 × 1 min at 80–85% HRmax (RPE 6–7/10), 1:2 work-to-rest ratio, session duration 15–20 min.
- MICT: 3 sessions/week, 20–30 min at 55–65% HRmax (RPE 11–13).

**Phase 2 (Weeks 5–8): Intensification**
- HIIT: 3 sessions/week, 4 × 2 min at 85–90% HRmax (RPE 7–8/10), 1:1 to 1:1.5 work-to-rest, session duration 20–25 min.
- MICT: 3–4 sessions/week, 30–40 min at 60–70% HRmax.

**Phase 3 (Weeks 9–16): Loading**
- HIIT: 3 sessions/week, 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax (RPE 8–9/10), 1:1 work-to-rest, session duration 30–40 min.
- SIT (optional): 1 × week replace HIIT with 4–6 × 30‑s all-out sprints, 4 min recovery, session duration 20–25 min.

**Phase 4 (Weeks 17–52+): Maintenance and periodic reloading**
- Maintain HIIT at 2–3 sessions/week, with "progressive overload" applied every 4–8 weeks (increase interval duration, reduce rest, or increase intensity).
- Consider periodisation: 4 weeks HIIT → 1 week MICT/recovery → 4 weeks HIIT.
- **Hybrid approach for long-term adherence:** 2 days/week HIIT (20–30 min) + 1 day/week MICT (30–45 min) may sustain engagement and prevent monotony.

**Evidence for long-term maintenance:**
- The Generation 100 study (Stensvold et al., 2020) showed that **twice-weekly HIIT over 5 years** sustained VO₂max improvements and reduced mortality in older adults.
- A 2025 study (NIH, Long-Term HIIT Follow-up) in adults with metabolic syndrome found that **4 months/year of 4 × 4 min HIIT over 8 years** increased VO₂max by 14% and power output by 4%, with no increase in medication use.
- The TREHIIT trial 2‑year follow-up (2025) found that while HIIT frequency declined, participants who maintained at least 1–2 sessions/week retained some metabolic benefits, and many transitioned to moderate-intensity exercise.

## 7. Moderators of Effect

### 7.1 Sex
- **Women may derive greater relative CRF benefits from HIIT** than men, possibly due to higher proportional area of slow-twitch fibres and greater fatigue resistance (Frontiers in Physiology, 2020).
- **Menopause status:** Postmenopausal women may require higher HIIT volumes to achieve similar CRF improvements as premenopausal women, due to oestrogen-dependent differences in vascular function.
- **Perceptual responses:** A 2024 study found no sex differences in enjoyment or perceived exertion to acute HIIT and SIT (PubMed, 2024), suggesting both sexes can tolerate and enjoy HIIT similarly.
- **Practical implication:** HIIT appears equally effective and enjoyable for both sexes, but women may benefit from slightly longer recovery periods between intervals.

### 7.2 Age Band (30–49 vs. 50–70)
- **Adults 30–49:** Typically have higher baseline fitness and can tolerate higher-intensity protocols (e.g., 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax, SIT). The 2023 meta-analysis of 29 RCTs found that age 18–45 years was associated with greater fat loss and CRF improvements from HIIT compared to MICT (PMC10048683, 2023).
- **Adults 50–70:** VO₂max declines by ~10% per decade after age 50. HIIT can attenuate this decline. A 2021 meta-analysis (Poon et al., *J Sports Sci*) in middle-aged and older adults found HIIT superior to MICT for CRF improvement (MD ∼ 1.5–2 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹). The 2026 meta-analysis of healthy elderly (Chu et al.) confirmed HIIT superiority (MD = 1.17 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ vs. MICT).
- **Older adults (≥65):** A 2026 analysis of 18 RCTs found HIIT feasible and safe, with retention rates of 59–100% and no increased adverse events (RR = 1.34, p = 0.13). HIIT improved depressive symptoms (SMD −0.19) and preserved cognitive function.
- **Practical recommendation:** For adults 50–70, start with lower-intensity HIIT (e.g., 1 min intervals at 80–85% HRmax) and gradually progress to 4 min intervals. Pre-exercise health screening is recommended.

### 7.3 Baseline Fitness
- **Sedentary/deconditioned individuals:** Tend to show the largest relative improvements in VO₂max and insulin sensitivity from HIIT, often exceeding those from MICT. The "low-responder" phenomenon to HIIT is less common than once thought; a 2011 meta-analysis (Bacon et al., *PLoS ONE*) found that all subjects improved VO₂max with HIIT when intervals were ≥3 min.
- **Recreationally active:** Both HIIT and MICT improve CRF, but HIIT provides additional benefits for VO₂peak and fat loss even in already-active individuals (Scientific Reports, 2025).
- **Low baseline fitness (<25 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹):** Start with very low-volume HIIT (e.g., 2 × 20‑s REHIT) or MICT (30 min at 55–60% HRmax) before progressing to standard HIIT.

### 7.4 Cardiometabolic Risk (Overweight/Obesity, Prediabetes/MetS, Hypertension)

**Overweight/Obesity:**
- HIIT is at least as effective as MICT for reducing body fat, waist circumference, and liver fat, and is more time-efficient (Deng et al., 2026; PMC10048683, 2023).
- The 2024 enjoyment meta-analysis specifically in overweight/obese adults found HIIT elicited higher enjoyment than MICT (Frontiers in Public Health, 2024).

**Prediabetes/Metabolic Syndrome:**
- HIIT produces larger reductions in HOMA-IR and fasting insulin than MICT (31% vs. 9% reduction in fasting insulin in the Gillen et al. 2016 SIT study).
- The 2024 American Diabetes Association Standards of Care recommend ≥150 min/week of moderate-to-vigorous activity for prediabetes, with HIIT as a time-efficient option.

**Hypertension:**
- HIIT and MICT have similar effects on resting BP, but HIIT is superior for reducing daytime ambulatory SBP (−4.14 mmHg) and improving endothelial function (FMD: +2.75%) (PMC9771301, 2022).
- In stage 1 hypertension (SBP 130–139 mmHg), HIIT reduced SBP by an additional −4.79 mmHg compared to MICT.

**NAFLD:**
- 12 weeks of HIIT reduced liver fat by 27% (from 11% to 8%) in NAFLD patients (Houghton et al., 2015), with comparable effects to MICT but requiring less time.
- The 2024 NMA found that combined AT + RT was best for overall NAFLD improvement, but HIIT ranked second for lipid outcomes.

### 7.5 Medication Status
- **Antihypertensives (beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers):** Beta-blockers blunt the heart rate response, making HR-based HIIT prescription unreliable. RPE-based prescription (RPE 6–8/10 for HIIT) is recommended for these patients.
- **Statins:** No contraindications; HIIT may improve lipid profile additively with statin therapy.
- **Metformin:** May blunt the acute CRF response to HIIT by reducing mitochondrial biogenesis, but chronic HIIT still improves insulin sensitivity and HbA1c.
- **Insulin/secretagogues:** Increased risk of hypoglycaemia during and after HIIT sessions; glucose monitoring and carbohydrate adjustment are recommended.
- **Oral corticosteroids:** May impair muscle adaptation to HIIT; caution with progression.
- **Anticoagulants:** No absolute contraindication, but increased risk of bleeding from falls; supervised HIIT with low-impact modalities (cycling, swimming) is recommended.

## 9. Recommendations for Clinical Practice

1. **For adults aged 30–70 with <150 min/week available:** HIIT (≥75 min/week) or SIT (≥30 min/week) is the preferred modality due to superior time efficiency and comparable or better outcomes for CRF, body composition, and insulin sensitivity compared to MICT.

2. **For adults with <60 min/week available:** SIT or LV-HIIT (2–3 × 10–20 min sessions/week) is the only evidence-based approach that can achieve clinically meaningful improvements. Even 30 min/week of SIT can improve VO₂peak by ~6 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹.

3. **For prediabetes/T2D:** HIIT is recommended as first-line exercise due to its large effect on HbA1c (−0.60%) and HOMA-IR, with a minimum of 75 min/week.

4. **For hypertension:** HIIT is equivalent to MICT for resting BP but superior for daytime ambulatory BP and endothelial function. RPE-based prescription is needed for patients on beta-blockers.

5. **For NAFLD:** HIIT (≥75 min/week) or combined AT+RT (≥150 min/week) is recommended. Both HIIT and MICT reduce liver fat similarly, but HIIT requires less time.

6. **For long-term maintenance (≥6–12 months):** A hybrid approach combining 2 days/week HIIT with 1 day/week MICT may sustain adherence better than HIIT alone. Periodic "reloading" phases (e.g., 4 weeks HIIT, 1 week MICT) prevent plateau. Home-based HIIT with wearable technology support is feasible for motivated individuals, but declining adherence over time should be anticipated and managed.

7. **Safety monitoring:** All adults ≥50 or with cardiometabolic risk factors should undergo pre-exercise health screening. HIIT should be initiated with medical clearance in patients with known CVD, uncontrolled hypertension, or T2D on insulin/secretagogues.
