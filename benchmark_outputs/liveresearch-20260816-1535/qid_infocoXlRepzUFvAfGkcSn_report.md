
# U.S. Banks (>$50B Assets) with Less-Than-Satisfactory CRA Ratings (2019–2024)

## Background

This report identifies U.S. banks with more than $50 billion in total assets that received a Community Reinvestment Act (CRA) rating of **"Needs to Improve"** or **"Substantial Noncompliance"** — the two less-than-satisfactory ratings — in their most recent evaluation during the period 2019–2024. Less-than-satisfactory ratings are exceptionally rare among large banks. According to NCRC analysis of the FFIEC CRA Ratings Database, only about 2% of all CRA exams in recent years result in a rating of "Needs to Improve" or "Substantial Noncompliance," and the vast majority of those are small, community institutions [NCRC analysis](https://ncrc.org/do-cra-ratings-reflect-differences-in-performance-an-examination-using-federal-reserve-data).

## Key Finding: One Bank Qualifies

Based on thorough review of OCC, Federal Reserve, and FDIC CRA evaluation databases, monthly examination lists, and press releases, **only one U.S. bank with assets over $50 billion received a less-than-satisfactory CRA rating in its most recent evaluation between 2019 and 2024:**

---

### City National Bank (Los Angeles, CA)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Bank Name** | City National Bank |
| **Headquarters** | Los Angeles, California |
| **Assets** | ~$98 billion (as of 2024–2026) |
| **Overall CRA Rating** | **Needs to Improve** |
| **Exam Date** | March 27, 2023 |
| **Regulator** | Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) |
| **Examination Type** | Large Bank |
| **Charter Number** | 14695 |

**Component Test Ratings:**
- **Lending Test:** Needs to Improve
- **Service Test:** Needs to Improve
- **Investment Test:** Outstanding

**Key reasons for the rating:** The OCC cited U.S. Department of Justice allegations of redlining by City National in Los Angeles County (2017–2020) as the primary contributing factor to the "Needs to Improve" rating on the lending test. The service test rating reflected concerns about branch distribution and accessibility. Despite the weak overall rating, the bank received "Outstanding" on the investment test, reflecting "excellent responsiveness to community needs."

- **Asset size:** City National Bank reports assets of approximately $98 billion. [About City National Bank](https://www.cnb.com/about-us.html)

## "Substantial Noncompliance" Ratings

No U.S. bank with assets over $50 billion received a **"Substantial Noncompliance"** CRA rating in the 2019–2024 period. This rating is exceedingly rare across all bank sizes. The FDIC's monthly lists of CRA-evaluated banks, which cover primarily smaller FDIC-supervised institutions, rarely show "Substantial Noncompliance" for any bank. When it does occur, it is typically at very small community banks. The Federal Reserve and OCC databases similarly show essentially no "Substantial Noncompliance" ratings for large banks in this period.

---

## Context: Rarity of Less-Than-Satisfactory Ratings

- According to NCRC, of the 48,842 CRA exams with exam dates after July 1997, only 899 (1.84%) resulted in an overall rating of "Needs to Improve" or "Substantial Noncompliance" [NCRC Analysis](https://ncrc.org/ncrcs-comment-on-proposal-to-repeal-2023-cra-rule).
- For exams from 2018 onward, only 160 out of 8,620 (1.86%) received less-than-satisfactory ratings.
- The vast majority of these less-than-satisfactory ratings are assigned to small community banks, not to large or regional banks with assets over $50 billion.
- No large bank over $50 billion received a "Substantial Noncompliance" rating during 2019–2024.
