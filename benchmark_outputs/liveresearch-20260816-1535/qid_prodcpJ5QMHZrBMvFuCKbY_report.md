
# Comprehensive Comparison of Major Payment Processors for U.S. E-Commerce (2026)

## Executive Summary

This report compares nine leading payment platforms—Stripe, PayPal, Square, Braintree, Adyen, Shopify Payments, 2Checkout (Verifone), Amazon Pay, and Apple Pay—across the eight dimensions most relevant to small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs) in the United States. All data is current as of mid-2026, drawn from official pricing pages, verified third-party analyses, and user review aggregators.

## 2. Detailed Analysis by Dimension

### 2.1 E-Commerce Integration Capabilities

**Stripe** leads the industry with its API-first approach, supporting 135+ currencies and 100+ payment methods through a single integration. It offers SDKs for 15+ programming languages, native plugins for Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento, and other major platforms, and a robust webhook system for event-driven automation. Its developer experience is widely considered best-in-class.

**PayPal** provides a quick, low-code integration via PayPal Checkout buttons and Smart Buttons. It integrates with virtually every e-commerce platform through plugins. However, customization is more limited than Stripe or Braintree, and the checkout flow redirects to PayPal's interface unless using Advanced Checkout (which still shows PayPal branding).

**Braintree** (a PayPal company) offers a developer-friendly API similar to Stripe but with deeper integration of PayPal and Venmo. Its Drop-in UI enables quick implementation with multiple payment methods. The documentation is thorough but considered less intuitive than Stripe's by many developers.

**Adyen** provides a single API that covers online, mobile, and in-store payments. It is the most comprehensive platform for omnichannel commerce but requires significant technical investment. It is best suited for mid-market to enterprise businesses.

**Shopify Payments** is the default payment processor for Shopify stores. It is deeply integrated into the Shopify admin, checkout, and reporting. For Shopify merchants, it offers the lowest total cost (no external gateway surcharge). For non-Shopify sites, it is not available.

**2Checkout** (Verifone) offers three integration tiers: 2Sell (basic checkout), 2Subscribe (recurring billing), and 2Monetize (full Merchant of Record). It integrates with 120+ shopping carts and provides a hosted checkout page, making it easy to set up but less customizable.

**Amazon Pay** provides a simple button integration that lets customers use their Amazon account credentials. It integrates with major platforms like Shopify, Magento, and WooCommerce. The checkout experience is familiar and trusted but limited to Amazon's ecosystem.

**Apple Pay** is not a standalone processor but a digital wallet that can be enabled through any card processor (Stripe, Square, Braintree, etc.). Integration is straightforward: merchants enable Apple Pay in their processor's dashboard and add a button to their checkout page.

### 2.2 Transaction Fees & Pricing Structures

**Stripe** charges a flat 2.9% + $0.30 for domestic online card transactions. International cards add 1.5%, and currency conversion adds 1%. ACH payments are 0.8% capped at $5. Chargebacks cost $15 (refunded if won). Volume discounts are available for businesses processing >$250,000 annually. Stripe Billing adds 0.7% of subscription volume. The effective rate for a typical SMB is 3.2%–4.4% depending on international mix.

**PayPal** charges 2.99% + $0.49 for standard card payments and 3.49% + $0.49 for PayPal Checkout and Venmo payments. PayPal Pay Later (BNPL) costs 4.99% + $0.49. International payments add 1.5%, and PayPal's currency conversion spread is 3%–4% above the mid-market rate, making it one of the most expensive options for cross-border transactions. Chargebacks cost $20. The effective rate can exceed 5.5% for international transactions.

**Square** raised its online rates in January 2026: Free plan users now pay 3.3% + $0.30 (up from 2.9% + $0.30). Plus ($49/mo) and Premium ($149/mo) plans pay 2.9% + $0.30 for online transactions. In-person rates are 2.6% + $0.15 (Free), 2.5% + $0.15 (Plus), and 2.4% + $0.15 (Premium). Afterpay costs 6% + $0.30. ACH bank transfers are 1% (min $1). Instant transfers cost 1.95%.

**Braintree** officially charges 2.89% + $0.29 for cards and digital wallets (as of May 2026), with PayPal transactions at 3.49% + $0.49 and Venmo at 3.5% + $0.49. ACH is 0.75% capped at $5. International cards add 1%, and currency conversion adds 1%. Chargebacks cost $15. Volume discounts are available for processing >$80,000/month.

**Adyen** uses Interchange++ pricing, which passes through the exact interchange and scheme fees plus a markup (typically €0.11 + 0.60% for Visa/Mastercard). The effective rate for a $100 US credit card transaction ranges from $2.40 to $3.75. Adyen requires a monthly minimum invoice (often €500–€1,000), and there is typically a setup fee of €500–€5,000. This makes Adyen unsuitable for small merchants.

**Shopify Payments** rates vary by plan: Basic (2.9% + $0.30), Grow (2.7% + $0.30), Advanced (2.5% + $0.30), and Plus (2.15% + $0.30). In-person rates are 2.6% + $0.10 (Basic) down to 2.4% + $0.10 (Advanced). If using a third-party processor, Shopify adds 2% (Basic), 1% (Grow), 0.6% (Advanced), or 0.2% (Plus) surcharge. Chargebacks cost $15 (refunded if won).

**2Checkout** (Verifone) charges 3.5% + $0.35 (2Sell), 4.5% + $0.45 (2Subscribe), or 6.0% + $0.60 (2Monetize) per successful sale. Cross-border transactions add 2% in specific countries. PayPal transactions add 3%. Chargebacks cost $20. Settlement takes 7–14 business days, significantly longer than competitors.

**Amazon Pay** charges 2.9% + $0.30 for domestic transactions. Cross-border processing adds 1%. Chargebacks cost $20 (refunded if won under Payment Protection Policy). There are no monthly fees.

**Apple Pay** does not charge merchants directly. Merchants pay their standard processor fees (e.g., 2.9% + $0.30 through Stripe). Apple charges a 0.15% fee to card issuers, not merchants.

### 2.3 International Payment Support & Multi-Currency Processing

**Stripe** is the strongest option for SMBs selling internationally. It supports 135+ presentment currencies, operates in 46 fully supported countries, and accepts payments from customers in 195+ countries. Multi-currency settlement is available in the US and other major markets, allowing merchants to hold and pay out in up to 18 currencies, avoiding FX fees.

**PayPal** operates in 200+ countries but only supports 25 currencies for holding balances. The currency conversion spread (3%–4%) is the highest among major processors. For businesses with significant international volume, this can add substantial costs.

**Square** is the weakest option for international sales. US-based Square accounts can only process in USD. Square is available in only 8 countries total. Customers outside the US can pay with internationally-issued cards, but the merchant cannot set prices in foreign currencies or settle in non-USD.

**Braintree** supports 130+ currencies in 44 countries. Multi-currency setup is available but requires approval from banking partners. International cards incur a 1% surcharge, and currency conversion adds 1%.

**Adyen** supports 150+ currencies with local acquiring in 29 markets. It offers like-for-like settlement (payouts in the same currency collected) in many regions. This is the most sophisticated international payment platform, but the high minimums and setup fees make it accessible only to larger merchants.

**Shopify Payments** supports 130+ currencies and is available in 39 countries. Multi-currency payouts (US, HK, SG) allow merchants to settle in multiple currencies and avoid conversion fees. International currency conversion costs 1.5% for US stores.

**2Checkout** supports 100+ currencies and 200+ countries. It offers multi-currency pricing and settlement (USD, GBP, EUR). Its Merchant of Record model handles global tax compliance, making it attractive for digital goods sellers who want to outsource complexity.

**Amazon Pay** supports 12 currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, JPY, CHF, DKK, HKD, NZD, NOK, SEK, ZAR). Cross-border transactions add 1%. The limited currency list constrains its usefulness for truly global sales.

**Apple Pay** works internationally wherever contactless payments are accepted. Currency conversion is handled by the card issuer, not Apple.

### 2.4 Ease of Setup & Technical Requirements

**Stripe** and **Square** are the easiest to set up. Both require no monthly fees and allow merchants to start accepting payments within minutes. Stripe requires some technical work for custom integrations but offers pre-built checkout pages. Square is ideal for non-technical users.

**PayPal** and **Amazon Pay** are also very easy to set up, requiring only a button or plugin integration. No coding is required for basic use.

**Braintree** and **Adyen** require significant technical expertise. Braintree's API is well-documented but more complex than Stripe's. Adyen requires a full-time development team and an enterprise-level contract.

**Shopify Payments** is the easiest option for Shopify merchants—it activates automatically when a store is created.

**2Checkout** is moderately easy to set up via its 120+ cart integrations, but custom API integrations require more effort.

### 2.5 Supported Payment Methods

**Digital Wallets**: All processors support Apple Pay and Google Pay (except 2Checkout, which supports PayPal and cards but not Apple Pay natively). Stripe and Adyen offer the broadest wallet coverage, including Alipay, WeChat Pay, Cash App Pay, GrabPay, and many regional wallets.

**Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL)**:
- **Stripe**: Klarna, Affirm, Afterpay
- **PayPal**: PayPal Pay Later (4.99% + $0.49)
- **Square**: Afterpay (6% + $0.30)
- **Braintree**: PayPal Pay Later (via PayPal integration)
- **Adyen**: Klarna, Afterpay, Affirm
- **Shopify Payments**: Shop Pay Installments (~5%–6%), plus PayPal integration
- **2Checkout**: Limited BNPL support
- **Amazon Pay**: Amazon Monthly Payments (limited)
- **Apple Pay**: BNPL through partner apps (e.g., Affirm, Klarna) using Apple Pay as the funding source

### 2.6 Fraud Protection & Security Features

**Stripe Radar** is the most comprehensive built-in fraud tool among the flat-rate processors. It uses machine learning trained on millions of transactions, offers customizable rules, risk scoring (0–99), adaptive 3D Secure, and blocklists. Radar for Fraud Teams costs $0.02–$0.07 per screened transaction. Stripe also offers Chargeback Protection (0.4% per transaction) for fraud-related disputes.

**PayPal** offers Seller Protection for eligible transactions (unauthorized payments and item-not-received claims). Its Fraud Protection tool (for Advanced Credit and Debit Card Payments) uses adaptive machine learning. However, Seller Protection has strict eligibility requirements (proof of shipment, signature confirmation for high-value items). The $20 chargeback fee is not refunded.

**Square** provides Risk Manager, which includes machine learning fraud detection, 3D Secure, AVS, and custom rules. Square does not offer a chargeback protection reimbursement program (it was discontinued). Dispute management is handled through a dashboard, but merchants bear the cost of fraud.

**Braintree** offers basic fraud tools (CVV, AVS) and a premium Fraud Protection Advanced tool that uses machine learning from PayPal's transaction data. It includes velocity checks, device fingerprinting, and 3D Secure. The $15 chargeback fee is standard.

**Adyen RevenueProtect** is the most sophisticated fraud prevention suite among the platforms reviewed. It combines machine learning, custom risk rules, device fingerprinting, backtesting, dynamic 3D Secure, and case management. It is available as a premium feature.

**Shopify Payments** includes fraud analysis (machine learning-based risk scoring), 3D Secure, and Shopify Protect (free chargeback protection for eligible Shop Pay orders). Shopify Protect covers fraud-related chargebacks on Shop Pay transactions if the merchant fulfills within 7 days with tracking.

**2Checkout** employs Verifone's risk management systems, including fraud detection, 3D Secure, and tokenization. It is PCI DSS Level 1 compliant.

**Amazon Pay** uses Amazon's fraud detection technology, which is the same system protecting Amazon.com. It offers a Payment Protection Policy that covers fraud-related chargebacks if the merchant meets eligibility requirements.

**Apple Pay** provides the strongest security foundation: tokenization (Device Account Number replaces the actual card number), Secure Enclave hardware security, and biometric authentication. For in-person transactions, there are effectively no chargebacks. For online transactions, chargeback liability depends on the underlying processor.

### 2.7 Customer Support Quality

**Stripe** offers 24/7 chat and email support with phone callback available. The documentation is excellent, and the developer community is large. However, user reviews on Trustpilot (1.6/5) and BBB highlight frequent complaints about account holds and slow resolution of complex issues.

**PayPal** provides phone, chat, and email support, but user satisfaction is low (Trustpilot 1.5/5). Common complaints include automated responses, difficulty reaching a human agent, and long resolution times for account limitations.

**Square** offers phone support during business hours (6am–6pm PT) for Free plan users and 24/7 phone support for Plus and Premium subscribers. Trustpilot rating is 1.5/5, with common complaints about account freezes and poor communication.

**Braintree** has the weakest support reputation. Support is primarily email-based, and Trustpilot reviews (1.5/5) frequently cite unresponsive support and account holds. Developer documentation is good but not as comprehensive as Stripe's.

**Adyen** provides 24/7 phone and email support with dedicated account managers for enterprise clients. G2 ratings are strong (4.0/5), but Trustpilot (1.3/5) reflects poor experiences from smaller merchants who struggle to get attention.

**Shopify Payments** offers 24/7 chat support for all plans, with phone support for Shopify Plus and Retail plans. The Shopify Community forum is a valuable resource. User reviews are mixed; common complaints include difficulty reaching a human agent for non-Plus merchants.

**2Checkout** provides 24/7 phone and email support with dedicated channels for technical support, chargebacks, and refunds. Live chat is available in the Merchant Control Panel.

**Amazon Pay** benefits from Amazon's customer service infrastructure, offering 24/7 phone and chat support for merchants. The support is generally reliable, though some merchants report difficulty navigating the support system for payment-specific issues.

**Apple Pay** does not offer merchant support—merchants must contact their payment processor (e.g., Stripe, Square) for Apple Pay-related issues.

### 2.8 Scalability for Small to Medium-Sized Businesses

**Stripe** is the most scalable option for SMBs. It has no monthly fees, no minimum processing volume, and offers volume discounts for businesses processing >$250,000 annually. As businesses grow, they can access custom pricing, dedicated support, and additional features (e.g., Stripe Connect for platforms, Stripe Issuing for card programs). The ability to expand to 46 countries and 135+ currencies without changing processors makes it ideal for growing businesses.

**PayPal** is widely used and trusted by consumers, making it a good option for new businesses. However, its high fees (especially for international and BNPL transactions) and risk of account holds can become problematic as businesses scale. The lack of volume discounts for most merchants means costs do not decrease with scale.

**Square** is excellent for micro-businesses and very small SMBs, especially those with in-person sales. However, the 2026 fee increases, limited international support, and lack of multi-currency processing make it less suitable for businesses that plan to scale internationally or process high online volumes.

**Braintree** is a good option for developers and businesses that want PayPal/Venmo integration without the fees of PayPal Checkout. It scales reasonably well, with volume discounts at >$80,000/month. However, poor customer support and account hold risks can be problematic at scale.

**Adyen** is not suitable for most SMBs due to its high monthly minimums, setup fees, and enterprise focus. It is only cost-effective for businesses processing >$250,000–$400,000 per month.

**Shopify Payments** is the best option for Shopify merchants. It scales well within the Shopify ecosystem, with lower processing rates on higher-tier plans. However, businesses that want to leave Shopify cannot take their payment processing with them.

**2Checkout** is a good option for digital goods and SaaS businesses selling globally, especially those that want to use the Merchant of Record model to offload tax compliance. However, the high transaction fees (3.5%–6.0%) and slow settlement times (7–14 days) can be challenging for cash flow.

**Amazon Pay** is easy to implement and trusted by consumers, but its limited currency support and dependence on the Amazon ecosystem limit its scalability for international growth.

**Apple Pay** is a complementary payment method, not a standalone processor. It is highly scalable because it can be added to any payment stack and works with any processor. However, it cannot serve as a business's primary payment solution.

## 4. Key Trends and Changes in 2026

1. **Square's 2026 Fee Increases**: Square raised online processing rates by 14% for Free plan users (from 2.9% to 3.3% + $0.30), making it less competitive for online-only businesses.

2. **Stripe's Radar Expansion**: Stripe launched Radar as a standalone product available to non-Stripe merchants, positioning it as a cross-platform fraud prevention layer.

3. **PayPal's Fee Restructuring**: PayPal updated its billing structure in April 2026, with new minimum transaction fees and Mastercard Digital Enablement Fee changes.

4. **Adyen's U.S. Growth**: Adyen continues to expand in North America, growing 26% in constant currency in 2025, but remains enterprise-focused.

5. **Shopify's Multi-Currency Payouts**: Shopify expanded multi-currency payouts to US, HK, and SG merchants, allowing them to settle in multiple currencies and avoid conversion fees.

6. **BNPL Mainstreaming**: The US BNPL market is projected to reach $128 billion in 2026, with all major processors now offering BNPL options (Klarna, Affirm, Afterpay, or proprietary solutions).

7. **Apple Pay Growth**: Apple Pay now accounts for 92% of US mobile wallet transactions, making it a must-have payment method for e-commerce merchants.
