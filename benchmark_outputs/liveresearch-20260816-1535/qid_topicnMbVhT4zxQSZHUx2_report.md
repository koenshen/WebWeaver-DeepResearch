## 1. Why Social Isolation Has Worsened Since 2020

### The Pre-COVID Baseline

Even before the COVID-19 pandemic, loneliness was a significant public health issue for older adults. According to a 2020 report from the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, approximately **24% of community-dwelling adults aged 65 and older** in the United States (about 7.7 million people) were socially isolated, and **4% (1.3 million)** were severely socially isolated. Using data from the Health and Retirement Study, researchers found that **43% of Americans aged 60 and older reported feeling lonely**. (Source: [PMC - National Academies Report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7437541))

### The Pandemic's Devastating Impact

The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically worsened these numbers:

- **Increased loneliness measurement**: A study published in the *Journals of Gerontology: Series B* found that older adults who implemented social distancing measures, including cancellation of social activities and avoiding contact with others, reported **up to 41% greater loneliness**. (Source: [Oxford Academic - COVID-19 Social Distancing Measures and Loneliness](https://academic.oup.com/psychsocgerontology/advance-article/doi/10.1093/geronb/gbab009/6082783))

- **Rural vs. urban disparities**: A scoping review published in *BMC Geriatrics* found that **53.4% of rural-based older adults** were lonely in 2020 compared to **43.7%** in urban areas. (Source: [Springer - Social isolation and loneliness among older adults living in rural areas](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-023-04196-3))

- **Latest AARP findings (2025)**: A 2025 AARP survey found that **40% of U.S. adults aged 45 and older now report being lonely**, a significant increase from 35% in both 2010 and 2018. This represents a persistent and growing problem. (Source: [AARP - Loneliness Among Adults Age 45-Plus Is Increasing](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/social-leisure/relationships/loneliness-social-connections-2025))

### Why It Happened

Several key factors converged to worsen isolation for seniors:

| Factor | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Stay-at-home orders** | Seniors were urged to shelter in place for extended periods, cutting off in-person social contact |
| **Fear of infection** | Even after restrictions lifted, many seniors remained cautious about gatherings |
| **Loss of social infrastructure** | Senior centers, community centers, faith institutions, and libraries closed or reduced programming |
| **Technology barriers** | Many older adults lacked the skills, devices, or internet access to shift to virtual connections |
| **Loss of loved ones** | The pandemic caused disproportionate mortality among older adults, leading to grief and loss of social networks |
| **Health decline** | Delayed medical care and reduced physical activity during the pandemic worsened health conditions that can contribute to isolation |

The National Institute on Aging (NIA) notes that as people age, they often find themselves spending more time alone due to health changes, loss of family and friends, hearing and vision loss, disability, trouble getting around, and financial struggles. The pandemic significantly amplified all of these factors. (Source: [NIA - Social isolation, loneliness in older people pose health risks](https://www.nia.nih.gov/news/social-isolation-loneliness-older-people-pose-health-risks))

## 2. How Loneliness Impacts Health and Mental Well-Being

### The Physical Health Risks

Loneliness and social isolation are not just emotional challenges — they have serious, measurable effects on physical health. The NIA has linked social isolation and loneliness to higher risks for multiple conditions:

| Health Risk | Magnitude of Risk | Source |
|-------------|-------------------|--------|
| **Heart disease & stroke** | 30% increased risk of incident coronary artery disease or stroke | [PMC - National Academies Report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7437541) |
| **Dementia** | 50% increased risk of developing dementia | [PMC - National Academies Report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7437541) |
| **All-cause mortality (social isolation)** | 35% increased likelihood | [PMC - Loneliness, social isolation, and living alone meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11750934) |
| **All-cause mortality (loneliness)** | 14% increased likelihood | [PMC - Loneliness, social isolation, and living alone meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11750934) |
| **All-cause mortality (living alone)** | 21% increased likelihood | [PMC - Loneliness, social isolation, and living alone meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11750934) |
| **High blood pressure** | Documented association | [NIA - Social isolation, loneliness in older people pose health risks](https://www.nia.nih.gov/news/social-isolation-loneliness-older-people-pose-health-risks) |
| **Weakened immune system** | Documented association | [NIA - Social isolation, loneliness in older people pose health risks](https://www.nia.nih.gov/news/social-isolation-loneliness-older-people-pose-health-risks) |
| **Obesity** | Documented association | [NIA - Social isolation, loneliness in older people pose health risks](https://www.nia.nih.gov/news/social-isolation-loneliness-older-people-pose-health-risks) |

### Mental Health and Cognitive Effects

Beyond physical health, the mental health toll is profound:

- **Depression and anxiety**: Research consistently shows that lonely older adults are at significantly higher risk for depression and anxiety disorders. (Source: [NIA - Loneliness and Social Isolation — Tips for Staying Connected](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/loneliness-and-social-isolation/loneliness-and-social-isolation-tips-staying-connected))

- **Accelerated cognitive decline**: A Frontiers in Psychology study using Health and Retirement Study data found that loneliness accelerated cognitive decline over a 12-year period, even after accounting for depressive symptoms and other relevant factors. (Source: [Frontiers - Loneliness and global cognitive functioning](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1344044/full))

- **Frailty and disability**: Increased social isolation has been consistently associated with increased risks of disability and functional decline. (Source: [PMC - Social Isolation Changes and Long-Term Outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11270134))

### The Surgeon General's Warning

In 2023, the U.S. Surgeon General issued an advisory declaring loneliness and isolation an epidemic, noting that the health impact of social disconnection is comparable to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. (Source: [HHS - Surgeon General's Advisory on Social Connection](https://www.hhs.gov/surgeongeneral/priorities/connection/index.html))

### Why This Matters for You

The good news is that these risks are not inevitable. **Social connection is a powerful protective factor.** Research consistently shows that having social connections can help you experience fewer mental and physical health issues as you grow older. The key is to be proactive.

## 3. Community Programs, Technologies, and Solutions

### A. Government-Funded and National Programs

#### Eldercare Locator
- **What it is**: A nationwide public service of the U.S. Administration for Community Living (ACL) that connects older adults and their caregivers to trustworthy local support resources.
- **How to access**: Call **1-800-677-1116** (Monday–Friday, 8 AM–9 PM ET) or visit [eldercare.acl.gov](https://eldercare.acl.gov).
- **What it offers**: Connects you to Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs), local senior centers, transportation services, meal programs, and social engagement opportunities.

#### Commit to Connect
- **What it is**: An ACL initiative focused on reaching socially isolated people with programs and resources that build social connections.
- **How to access**: [acl.gov/CommitToConnect](https://acl.gov/CommitToConnect)
- **What it offers**: Articles, toolkits, web recordings, and a database of social engagement activities — many available both in-person and online.

#### AmeriCorps Seniors
- **What it is**: A federal program that matches adults 55 and older with volunteer opportunities that match their abilities and interests.
- **How to access**: Call **1-800-942-2677** or visit [nationalservice.gov/programs/senior-corps](https://www.nationalservice.gov/programs/senior-corps)
- **What it offers**: Programs like Foster Grandparents, Senior Companions, and RSVP (Retired and Senior Volunteer Program) that give you a purpose and a community.

### B. Nonprofit and Advocacy Organizations

#### AARP Foundation's Connect2Affect
- **What it is**: A comprehensive online resource designed to help older adults assess their social isolation risk and find local support.
- **How to access**: [connect2affect.org](https://connect2affect.org)
- **What it offers**:
  - A self-assessment tool to check your risk level
  - A searchable directory of programs and services in your area
  - "8 Ways to Build Social Connections" tips
  - Resources for making new friends and using technology

#### AARP Friendly Voice
- **What it is**: A volunteer-based phone call program where trained volunteers are available to talk with older adults.
- **How to access**: Visit [aarp.org](https://www.aarp.org) or call **1-888-687-2277**

#### Senior Planet from AARP / OATS
- **What it is**: The flagship program of Older Adults Technology Services (OATS) from AARP, offering free technology training for older adults.
- **How to access**: [seniorplanet.org](https://seniorplanet.org) or call a local Senior Planet center
- **What it offers**:
  - **Free live online classes** on Zoom: smartphone apps, online banking, telemedicine, social media, fitness, meditation, and more
  - **In-person centers** in Denver, Miami, Plattsburgh (NY), and other locations
  - **On-demand learning** videos and tutorials
  - **Affinity groups** for discussion and friendship
  - **Technology-themed community spaces** for older adults

> **Success Story**: Tom Kamber, executive director of OATS, notes that the live virtual experience during the pandemic was "very compelling" to participants. Students report that they benefit from asking questions in a live class setting, which is much more effective for many older adults than watching recorded videos. (Source: [AARP - Senior Planet and AARP Offer Free Online App Classes](https://www.aarp.org/personal-technology/senior-planet-and-aarp-online-classes))

#### Institute on Aging Friendship Line
- **What it is**: A 24/7 toll-free emotional support line for older adults, founded in 1973.
- **How to access**: Call **1-888-670-1360** (24/7, toll-free in California)
- **What it offers**: A compassionate listener who is trained to support older adults experiencing loneliness, grief, or emotional distress. The program also offers grief support groups.

#### Meals on Wheels America
- **What it is**: A nationwide network of community-based programs that deliver nutritious meals to older adults.
- **How to access**: Call **1-888-998-6325** or visit [mealsonwheelsamerica.org](https://www.mealsonwheelsamerica.org)
- **What it offers**: More than just meals — the friendly visit from a volunteer delivery driver is a critical social connection for many homebound seniors.

#### National Council on Aging (NCOA)
- **What it is**: A national organization that works with nonprofit organizations, governments, and businesses to provide community programs and services.
- **How to access**: [ncoa.org](https://www.ncoa.org) or call **1-571-527-3900**
- **What it offers**: Resources on social isolation, online therapy guides, intergenerational connection programs, and fall prevention programs that combine social engagement with physical health.

#### SilverSneakers
- **What it is**: A fitness program for older adults, often included with Medicare Advantage plans.
- **How to access**: Call **1-866-584-7389** or visit [silversneakers.com](https://www.silversneakers.com)
- **What it offers**: Free gym memberships, group fitness classes (both in-person and online), and a community of active older adults.

### C. Technology Solutions and Digital Inclusion

#### Bridging the Digital Divide

The digital divide remains a significant barrier for many older adults. According to Pew Research Center, as of 2025, approximately **10% of adults 65 and older** do not use the internet at all. (Source: [Syracuse University - What Is the Digital Divide?](https://ischool.syracuse.edu/what-is-the-digital-divide))

Here are practical technology solutions:

| Solution | Description | How to Access |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Senior Planet free classes** | Live Zoom classes on using smartphones, tablets, and apps | [seniorplanet.org/classes](https://seniorplanet.org/classes) |
| **Public library digital literacy workshops** | Free in-person and online classes on email, online safety, social media | Visit your local public library |
| **Local senior centers** | Many offer free or low-cost computer classes | Call your local Area Agency on Aging via the Eldercare Locator |
| **Affordable Connectivity Program (ACP)** | Federal program that provides discounts on internet service (note: funding status may vary) | Visit [fcc.gov/acp](https://www.fcc.gov/acp) |
| **Tablet lending programs** | Some libraries and nonprofits lend tablets pre-loaded with apps for staying connected | Check with your local library or senior center |

#### Specific Technologies for Staying Connected

- **Video calling apps**: Zoom, FaceTime, Skype, and Google Meet allow face-to-face conversations from home. Senior Planet offers free classes on how to use these.
- **Social media**: Facebook, Nextdoor, and local community groups can help you find events and reconnect with old friends.
- **Health and fitness apps**: SilverSneakers GO, Yoga with Adriene on YouTube, and other free fitness apps combine movement with community.
- **Audio-only options**: If video is overwhelming, phone calls and audio-only apps like Clubhouse can provide meaningful connection.

### D. Grassroots and Community-Based Programs

#### Senior Centers
- **What they are**: Local hubs that offer meals, exercise classes, educational programs, social activities, and volunteer opportunities.
- **How to find one**: Call the Eldercare Locator at **1-800-677-1116** or search online for "senior center near me."
- **Success story**: Many senior centers across the country pivoted during the pandemic to offer outdoor programming, phone-based check-ins, and virtual classes. Post-pandemic, many have returned to in-person activities while maintaining online options.

#### Foster Grandparent Program (AmeriCorps Seniors)
- **What it is**: A program that pairs adults 55+ with children and youth in schools, hospitals, and other settings.
- **How to access**: Call **1-800-942-2677** or visit [nationalservice.gov](https://www.nationalservice.gov/programs/senior-corps)
- **Why it works**: Intergenerational connections provide a powerful sense of purpose and belonging. The NCOA highlights that intergenerational connections can ease loneliness in older adults. (Source: [NCOA - How Intergenerational Connections Can Ease Loneliness](https://www.ncoa.org/article/how-intergenerational-connections-can-ease-loneliness-in-older-adults))

#### Friendly Visitor Programs
- **What they are**: Programs that match volunteers with older adults for regular in-person or phone visits.
- **Examples**: 
  - **Connected Companions Program** (Washtenaw County, MI): A friendly visitor program that provides companionship to older adults
  - **SAGEConnect**: Pairs volunteers with LGBTQ+ older adults for regular phone or video calls. (Source: [SAGEConnect](https://www.sageusa.org))

#### ARTZ Philadelphia
- **What it is**: A nonprofit that connects people living with dementia and their care partners to cultural experiences like museums, galleries, and concerts.
- **Why it works**: Creativity and shared cultural experiences provide meaningful social engagement. (Source: [Sarah Ralston Foundation - Programs for Seniors](https://sarahralstonfoundation.org/2025/01/14/programs-for-seniors-provide-relief-from-loneliness-and-social-isolation))

#### Faith-Based Programs
- Many churches, synagogues, mosques, and other faith communities have active older adult ministries, including:
  - **Phone tree systems** for daily/weekly check-ins
  - **Transportation services** to bring seniors to services and events
  - **Bible study groups, book clubs, and social events** both in-person and online

## 4. Practical Steps to Protect Yourself and Support Other Seniors

### What You Can Do for Yourself

#### Step 1: Assess Your Risk
Use the self-assessment tool on **Connect2Affect** ([connect2affect.org](https://connect2affect.org)) to gauge your level of social isolation. The NIA also provides a self-assessment in its Social Isolation and Loneliness Outreach Toolkit. (Source: [NIA - Loneliness and Social Isolation — Tips for Staying Connected](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/loneliness-and-social-isolation/loneliness-and-social-isolation-tips-staying-connected))

#### Step 2: Build a Daily Social Routine
Incorporate at least one social outlet into your daily routine. The University of Michigan's National Poll on Healthy Aging recommends:
- A regular phone call or video chat with a family member or friend at the same time each day
- A daily walk with a neighbor or friend
- A standing weekly lunch date or coffee meet-up
- Joining a morning exercise class, either in-person or online (Source: [Michigan Medicine - 8 ways older adults can reduce loneliness](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/8-ways-older-adults-can-reduce-loneliness-and-social-isolation-0))

#### Step 3: Learn One New Technology
Even if you're not tech-savvy, sign up for a free Senior Planet class or a class at your local public library. Start with:
- How to make a video call on your smartphone or tablet
- How to send an email
- How to join a free Zoom group (like a book club or exercise class)

#### Step 4: Find a Purpose Through Volunteering
Volunteering provides structure, purpose, and social connection. Options include:
- **AmeriCorps Seniors**: Read to children, help other seniors, or assist with tax preparation (Source: [NIA - Loneliness and Social Isolation — Tips for Staying Connected](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/loneliness-and-social-isolation/loneliness-and-social-isolation-tips-staying-connected))
- **AARP Foundation Tax-Aide**: Help fellow seniors prepare their taxes
- **Local food bank, animal shelter, or hospital**: Most need volunteers of all ages

#### Step 5: Consider a Pet
For those who can care for one, research shows that the companionship formed with pets improves both physical and mental health. The National Poll on Healthy Aging found that pet ownership is associated with lower levels of loneliness. (Source: [Michigan Medicine - 8 ways older adults can reduce loneliness](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/8-ways-older-adults-can-reduce-loneliness-and-social-isolation-0))

#### Step 6: Join a Group Activity
Research shows that **group-based programs** are particularly effective at reducing loneliness. Look for:
- **Book clubs** at your local library or senior center
- **Fitness classes** through SilverSneakers or the YMCA
- **Art classes** at a community center or museum
- **Game nights** (bingo, cards, board games) at a senior center
- **Walking groups** organized by local parks and recreation departments

#### Step 7: Address Health Barriers
- **Hearing loss**: If you have trouble hearing, get a hearing test and consider hearing aids. The NIA notes that people with untreated hearing loss may become depressed or withdrawn. (Source: [NIA - Loneliness and Social Isolation — Tips for Staying Connected](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/loneliness-and-social-isolation/loneliness-and-social-isolation-tips-staying-connected))
- **Mobility issues**: Explore transportation options through your local Area Agency on Aging, including senior shuttle services, paratransit, and volunteer driver programs.

#### Step 8: Seek Professional Help if Needed
If feelings of loneliness are accompanied by depression, anxiety, or thoughts of self-harm, reach out to:
- Your primary care provider
- A geriatric psychiatrist or licensed clinical social worker
- The **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (call or text 988)
- The **Friendship Line** at **1-888-670-1360**

### What You Can Do to Support Other Seniors

#### Become a Volunteer
- **Call a lonely senior**: Programs like the **Institute on Aging Friendship Line** and **SAGEConnect** need volunteers to make regular phone calls to isolated older adults.
- **Deliver meals**: **Meals on Wheels** programs always need volunteer drivers. The friendly visit is often as important as the meal.
- **Teach technology**: Volunteer to help other seniors learn to use their smartphones and tablets at a local senior center or through **Senior Planet**.
- **Become a Senior Companion**: Through **AmeriCorps Seniors**, you can be paired with a homebound senior for regular visits.

#### Start a Simple Program in Your Community
- **Phone tree**: Organize a group of neighbors to call each other daily or weekly
- **Walking group**: Start a "walking school bus" for seniors in your neighborhood
- **Shared meal**: Host a weekly potluck or coffee hour at a local community center, church, or library
- **Neighborhood check-in**: Simply knock on a neighbor's door or call someone you haven't heard from in a while

#### Advocate for Systemic Change
- Support the **Improving Measurements for Loneliness and Isolation Act**, which AARP backs. This bill aims to develop standardized definitions and measurements for loneliness and isolation, paving the way for effective solutions. (Source: [AARP - AARP Backs Federal Bill to Combat Loneliness](https://www.aarp.org/advocacy/improving-measurements-loneliness-isolation-2025))
- Contact your local elected officials to advocate for funding for senior centers, transportation services, and digital literacy programs.

### Success Stories

#### Mary's Journey (72 years old)
After losing her husband, Mary struggled with loneliness and rarely left home. Through therapy, she learned coping strategies, joined a virtual book club, and started attending local senior art classes. She now looks forward to weekly group sessions with peers and has made several close friends. (Source: [Mindful Health - Therapy for Older Adults](https://mindful.health/overcoming-loneliness-older-adults))

#### The Rural Senior Center Pivot
During the pandemic, many rural senior centers pivoted to offer outdoor programming, phone-based check-ins, and virtual classes. Some centers now maintain hybrid models, offering both in-person and online options, ensuring that seniors with mobility or transportation barriers can still participate. (Source: [BMC Geriatrics - Social isolation among older adults in rural areas](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-023-04196-3))

#### Senior Planet's Virtual Community
During the pandemic, Senior Planet moved entirely to live virtual programming, attracting thousands of older adults across the country. Participants formed lasting friendships through discussion groups and affinity groups, with many reporting that the live classes were more engaging and effective than pre-recorded tutorials. (Source: [AARP - Senior Planet and AARP Offer Free Online App Classes](https://www.aarp.org/personal-technology/senior-planet-and-aarp-online-classes))

## Final Thoughts

Loneliness and social isolation are serious health risks, but they are **not inevitable**. The evidence is clear: staying socially connected is one of the most powerful things you can do for your health and well-being as you age. Whether through picking up the phone, joining a free online class, volunteering, or simply walking next door to say hello to a neighbor, every small step toward connection matters.

**You are not alone.** The resources, programs, and people described in this report are ready to help. Start with one call to the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116, or visit one of the websites listed above. Your health — and your happiness — are worth it.
