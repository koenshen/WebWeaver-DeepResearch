
# GDPR Fines > €50 Million — Final Decisions (Calendar Years 2022–2026)

This report lists all **final decisions** by EU/EEA data protection authorities (DPAs) that imposed administrative fines exceeding **€50 million** between **1 January 2022 and 17 August 2026**. Each entry includes the organisation, fine amount, decision date, a summary of the infringement, and links to the official DPA decision and to an independent privacy-law tracker.  

Fines that were **proposed but not yet finalised** have been excluded, as have fines that were **annulled on appeal** (for example, the Amazon €746 million fine, which was annulled by the Luxembourg Administrative Court in March 2026). Where a fine is **under appeal** but remains a *final DPA decision* (i.e., the DPA has issued its final decision and the appeal is pending), the entry is included and the appeal status is noted.

## 1. Meta Platforms Ireland Limited — €1,200,000,000

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Organisation** | Meta Platforms Ireland Limited (Facebook) |
| **Fine amount** | €1,200,000,000 |
| **Date of decision** | 12 May 2023 |
| **DPA** | Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC) |
| **Sector** | Media, Telecoms and Broadcasting (Tech/Social Media) |

## 2. TikTok Technology Limited — €530,000,000

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Organisation** | TikTok Technology Limited |
| **Fine amount** | €530,000,000 (€485M for transfer violations + €45M for transparency failures) |
| **Date of decision** | 2 May 2025 |
| **DPA** | Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC) |
| **Sector** | Media, Telecoms and Broadcasting (Tech/Social Media) |
| **Infringement summary** | TikTok unlawfully transferred personal data of EEA users to China without adequate safeguards under Chapter V GDPR. The DPC also found TikTok failed to provide sufficient information in its privacy notice about the transfers (Article 13(1)(f) GDPR). TikTok had represented during the inquiry that no EEA user data was stored in China, but later disclosed that limited data had in fact been stored on Chinese servers. |
| **Appeal status** | Under appeal. TikTok filed an appeal in the Irish High Court in May 2025. In November 2025 the High Court granted a stay on the suspension order but upheld the fine. The Irish Supreme Court in April 2026 confirmed that the stay remains in place while the substantive challenge proceeds. |
| **DPA decision link** | [DPC press release (2 May 2025)](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following) |
| **Independent tracker link** | [enforcementtracker.com – ETid-2780](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/ETid-2780) |

## 4. Meta Platforms, Inc. (Instagram) — €405,000,000

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Organisation** | Meta Platforms, Inc. (Instagram) |
| **Fine amount** | €405,000,000 |
| **Date of decision** | 5 September 2022 |
| **DPA** | Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC) |
| **Sector** | Media, Telecoms and Broadcasting (Tech/Social Media) |
| **Infringement summary** | The DPC investigated how Instagram processed children’s data. It found that business-account settings allowed 13–17-year-old users to publicly display their phone numbers and email addresses. The DPC also found that Meta relied on contractual necessity as a legal basis for processing children’s data for personalised advertising, in violation of Articles 6(1) and 5(1)(a) GDPR (lawfulness, fairness and transparency). The EDPB issued a binding decision under Article 65 GDPR directing the DPC to expand its findings. |
| **Appeal status** | Under appeal by Meta. Unusually, the DPC also appealed the EDPB’s binding decision, arguing that the EDPB overreached its authority. |
| **DPA decision link** | [DPC press release (15 September 2022)](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/data-protection-commission-announces-decision-instagram-inquiry) |

## 8. Uber Technologies Inc. & Uber B.V. — €290,000,000

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Organisation** | Uber Technologies Inc. and Uber B.V. |
| **Fine amount** | €290,000,000 |
| **Date of decision** | 22 July 2024 |
| **DPA** | Dutch Data Protection Authority (Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, AP) |

| **Infringement summary** | Uber transferred personal data of European taxi drivers to the United States without implementing appropriate safeguards under Chapter V GDPR (Articles 44–46). ## Key Observations

- **The Irish DPC dominates enforcement**, issuing 9 of the 13 fines above (all but the two CNIL fines, the Dutch Uber fine, the Dutch Yango fine, and the Italian Enel fine).
- **Big Tech is the primary target**, but the list includes non-tech sectors: energy (Enel), transportation (Uber, Yango), and retail (Shein).
- **Cross-border data transfers** (Chapter V GDPR) drove the three highest fines: Meta €1.2B (EU→US), TikTok €530M (EU→China), and Uber €290M (EU→US).
- **Most fines are under appeal**, reflecting the high stakes involved. The user should verify current appeal status before relying on the finality of any particular fine.
