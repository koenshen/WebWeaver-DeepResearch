

# The Electric Truck Market: Comprehensive Research Report (2025–2026)

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The global electric truck market is experiencing a period of rapid, albeit uneven, expansion. In 2025, global sales of electric trucks (including medium- and heavy-duty freight trucks) exceeded **400,000 units for the first time**, doubling year-on-year and reaching **~9% of all truck sales** worldwide (IEA *Global EV Outlook 2026*). The light-duty pickup segment—encompassing consumer and commercial electric pickups—is valued at roughly **$5–6 billion in 2025**, growing to **$8.5 billion in 2026** at a CAGR of ~45% (Research and Markets, Jan 2026). Meanwhile, broader market definitions that include medium- and heavy-duty trucks place the total market size at **$80–84 billion in 2025**, projected to reach **$123 billion in 2026** (GM Insights). The market is driven by tightening emissions regulations, corporate fleet decarbonization targets, falling battery costs, and the expansion of charging infrastructure, though headwinds include the expiration of the US federal EV tax credit and policy uncertainty in some regions.

### 2.2 Demand Trajectory

- **Global unit sales of electric trucks** (medium and heavy freight) more than **doubled in 2025**, exceeding 400,000 units for the first time (IEA).
- **China** remains the dominant market, with electric trucks capturing **~25% of new truck sales in 2025**. For the first time, electric truck sales in China **outpaced LNG truck sales** (IEA).
- **Europe**: Electric truck sales grew ~40% year-on-year to ~17,000 units (MFT and HFT); electric MFTs reached 7% share, HFTs ~1.5% (IEA).
- **North America**: More than **38,000 medium- and heavy-duty electric trucks** on US roads by end of 2025 (EDF Energy Exchange).
- **Light-duty pickup segment**: Sales of electric pickups in the US (Rivian R1T, Ford F-150 Lightning, GM Silverado EV/Sierra EV, Tesla Cybertruck) totaled ~**85,000–90,000 units in 2025**, down from ~95,000 in 2024 due to policy headwinds.

### 2.3 Key Growth Drivers

1. **Falling battery costs**: Lithium-ion battery pack prices declined significantly in 2024–2025, improving total cost of ownership (TCO) for fleet operators.
2. **Regulatory tailwinds**: EU CO₂ standards for heavy-duty vehicles, California’s Advanced Clean Trucks (ACT) rule, and China’s truck scrappage programs.
3. **Corporate sustainability goals**: Major logistics firms (DHL, Amazon, UPS, FedEx) have committed to electrifying significant portions of their fleets.
4. **Expanding charging infrastructure**: Public charging connectors grew 28% in 2025 to 6.7 million globally (BloombergNEF); Megawatt Charging System (MCS) saw first public deployments in Europe (Aug 2025) and North America (Mar 2026).

## 4. Customer Segments Driving Growth

### 4.1 Commercial Fleets (Primary Driver)

Commercial fleets account for **~55% of electric truck demand** by value. Key drivers:

- **Amazon**: Over **40,000 Rivian EDVs** deployed globally by mid-2026, with a target of 100,000 by 2030 (Rivian Q2 2026 earnings; InsideEVs).
- **DHL**: Aiming to electrify 66% of last-mile fleet by 2030; deployed Mercedes eActros 300s and piloted Rivian vans.
- **UPS, FedEx, Walmart**: Active pilot programs and orders for medium-duty electric trucks.
- **Fleet TCO advantages**: Electric trucks offer 15–30% lower energy costs on predictable routes; maintenance savings of 30–50% (Congruence Market Insights).

### 4.2 Government & Municipal

- **12% of market revenue** in 2025 (Acumen).
- School buses, refuse trucks, municipal utility fleets, and postal services.
- California’s ACT rule requires increasing percentages of ZEV truck sales from 2024 onward.
- US federal funding: $7.5 billion from NEVI program; Germany approved €1.6 billion for truck charging (IEA).

### 4.3 Consumer/Retail

- Light-duty electric pickups (R1T, F-150 Lightning, Silverado EV, Cybertruck) address the consumer market.
- Consumer demand softened in 2025 after the expiration of the $7,500 federal tax credit in the US.
- Early adopters are primarily affluent, tech-forward buyers in suburban and rural areas with home charging access.
- The **Rivian R2** (starting at $45,000, deliveries from June 2026) is expected to broaden the consumer base.

## 5. Infrastructure and Regulatory Factors

### 5.1 Charging Infrastructure

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 (Estimated) |
|--------|------|------|------------------|
| Global public charging connectors | ~5.2M | 6.7M (+28%) | ~8.0M (+19%) (BloombergNEF) |
| US DC fast chargers added | 13,700 ports | 17,500 ports (+28%) | Continued growth |
| MCS (Megawatt Charging) | None | First public session (Europe, Aug 2025) | N. America first session (Mar 2026); Germany 124-site plan approved |

**Key developments:**
- **MCS**: Up to 1.5 MW charging; can charge a Class 8 truck from 20–80% in under 30 minutes (vs. 2–4 hours with CCS). Kempower, ABB, and others deploying MCS hardware.
- **Depot charging**: Dominant today; dynamic load balancing critical for managing multiple simultaneous sessions.
- **Tesla Megacharger network**: 46 stations planned for Tesla Semi, using MCS standard.
- **Germany**: €1.6 billion approved for truck charging; 124 sites with 836 charging points (447 MCS, 389 CCS) by 2030 (July 2026).

### 5.2 Regulatory Environment

| Region | Key Policies | Impact |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **United States** | IRA 45W tax credit **expired** end of 2025; NEVI funding continues; California ACT rule | Sales dipped in Q1 2026; tax credit loss reduced consumer demand |
| **European Union** | HDV CO₂ standards (45% reduction by 2030); road toll exemptions for BEVs | Strong growth; 40% sales increase in 2025 |
| **China** | Truck scrappage subsidies; zero-emission mandates in major cities | 25% EV share of new truck sales; electric trucks outsold LNG trucks |
| **Germany** | €1.6B truck charging fund; toll exemptions for BEVs | 4,400 electric trucks sold in 2025 (+30% YoY) |

### 5.3 Barriers to Adoption

- **Purchase price**: Electric trucks remain 2–3x more expensive than diesel equivalents (IEA).
- **Charging infrastructure gaps**: Especially for long-haul and rural routes.
- **Range and payload trade-offs**: Battery weight reduces payload capacity.
- **Policy uncertainty**: US federal tax credit expiration; potential changes to NEVI funding.
- **Grid capacity**: Depot charging for 20–50 trucks requires significant grid upgrades.

## 7. Technology Differentiators and Competitive Advantage

### 7.1 Rivian: Software-Defined and Vertical Integration

- **Quad-motor and tri-motor platforms**: Independent wheel torque control for exceptional off-road capability.
- **Rivian Autonomy Platform**: Third-generation autonomy system with proprietary RAP1 processor; “eyes-off” Level 4 capability targeted for future.
- **Rivian R2 and R2 Autonomy+**: Launch Package includes Autonomy+; configurator open June 2026.
- **Rivian Commercial Van (RCV)**: Designed from ground up for delivery; 1 billion cumulative miles driven (Q2 2026).
- **Volkswagen joint venture**: $5.8B investment (2024); 12.3% stake; access to VW’s scale and supply chain.
- **Charging network**: ~895 charging stalls (RAN); expanding.
- **Key vulnerability**: Still not profitable; net loss of $3.585B in 2025; heavily dependent on Amazon EDV orders.

### 7.2 Ford: Legacy Scale, but EV Setback

- **Ford Pro**: Comprehensive fleet management platform (telematics, charging, service).
- **BlueCruise**: Hands-free highway driving system; integrated with Apple Maps for 2026 Fathom.
- **F-150 Lightning**: Discontinued December 2025 due to inability to manufacture profitably.
- **Ford Fathom**: $30,000 EV pickup announced for 2027; built on new “Universal Electric Vehicle” platform.
- **Strategic partnership** with Renault for commercial EVs in Europe.
- **Key vulnerability**: Missed 2M EV target; lost billions on Lightning; retreating from EV truck segment.

### 7.3 GM: Ultium Platform and Range Leadership

- **Ultium platform**: Modular battery system (50–200 kWh); wireless battery management (wBMS); 800V architecture.
- **Range record**: Silverado EV WT achieved 1,059 miles on a single charge in controlled test; EPA-estimated 493 miles for Max pack.
- **Scalability**: Ultium powers Silverado EV, Sierra EV, Hummer EV, Escalade IQ, and BrightDrop vans.
- **GM Envolve**: Commercial fleet solutions division.
- **Future**: Moving beyond Ultium branding; partnerships with Forge Nano for next-gen batteries; LFP cells in development.
- **Key vulnerability**: Production paused at Factory Zero; EV sales down 43% in Q4 2025; $6B writedown.

### 7.4 Ram/Stellantis: Range-Extender Strategy

- **STLA Frame platform**: Dedicated EV platform for full-size trucks.
- **Ram 1500 Ramcharger (PHEV)**: 92 kWh battery + 3.6L V6 generator; 145 miles EV range; 690 miles total.
- **Ram 1500 REV (BEV)**: Delayed to 2027; 168 kWh (350 mi) or 229 kWh (500 mi) battery options.
- **Retail focus**: No major fleet contracts; targeting traditional Ram truck buyers.
- **Key vulnerability**: Multiple delays; BEV version canceled and reintroduced; losing market positioning.

## 10. Outlook and Key Takeaways

1. **The electric truck market is bifurcated**: Rapid growth in medium- and heavy-duty commercial segments (driven by regulation and TCO), while the light-duty consumer pickup segment faces headwinds from tax credit expiration and high prices.

2. **China dominates**: 80%+ of global electric truck sales; 25% EV share of new trucks in 2025; policy-driven.

3. **The US market is in transition**: The expiration of the $7,500 tax credit caused a sharp contraction in Q1 2026. However, fleet commitments (Amazon, DHL, USPS) provide a floor for demand.

4. **Rivian vs. Ford vs. GM vs. Ram**: Rivian leads in innovation and fleet integration but remains unprofitable. Ford has exited the electric pickup market (for now). GM has the strongest range and platform but has scaled back production. Ram has yet to deliver a single electric truck.

5. **Infrastructure is the next frontier**: MCS deployment in 2026–2027 will unlock long-haul electric trucking. Depot charging and grid upgrades are critical bottlenecks.

6. **The R2 moment**: Rivian’s R2 (starting at $45,000) and Ford’s Fathom ($30,000, 2027) represent the next wave of affordable electric trucks that could broaden the consumer market.
